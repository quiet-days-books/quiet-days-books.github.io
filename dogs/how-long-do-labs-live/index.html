In short

  • In the largest study so far, 43,428 Labradors in the UK had a median lifespan of 13.1 years, above the 11.9 average for large breeds.
  • UK vet-record studies give a little less: a median of 12.0 years in 2013 data and a life expectancy at birth of 11.77 years in 2016 to 2020 data.
  • Chocolate Labs lived a median of 10.7 years against 12.1 for other colours in one study, but only 34 chocolate deaths were counted.
  • Lean Labs live longer: overweight Labs had shorter median lifespans in a 2019 study, and a lifelong feeding study found the same.
  • By the AAHA 25% rule, a Labrador is a senior from about 9.8.

Labrador Retrievers usually live about 12 to 13 years. The largest study so far, of 43,428 UK Labradors, found a median lifespan of 13.1 years, so half lived longer than that. UK vet records give slightly lower figures, around 12 years, and the American Kennel Club lists 11 to 13.

How long do labs live on average?

Three UK studies have measured it, and they land between about 11.8 and 13.1 years:

The numbers measure slightly different things. A median is the age by which half the dogs have died. Life expectancy at birth is an average, and an average is pulled down by the dogs that die young. The 2024 study is also far bigger and pooled records from 18 sources, including insurers, breed registries, rehoming charities and vet groups, while the VetCompass studies read the notes of first-opinion vet practices.

In the 2018 study, half of the Labs that died were between 9.9 and 13.8 years old, and the oldest was 16.

Do labs live long for their size?

Yes. Bigger dogs usually die younger, and in the 2024 study the large purebreds as a group had a median of 11.9 years. Labradors beat that by more than a year and also beat crossbreeds (12.0) and the average for all dogs (12.5).

The study also gives a hazard ratio against crossbreeds. For Labs it was 0.8, meaning that at any given age a Lab's risk of dying was about a fifth lower than a crossbreed's. Our page on how long Golden Retrievers live covers the Lab's closest relative, and how long dogs live has the picture across all breeds and sizes.

Do chocolate labs live shorter lives?

In the one study that checked, yes. In the 2018 VetCompass study, chocolate Labradors that died had a median age of 10.7 years, against 12.1 for black and yellow Labs. The difference was statistically significant.

It rests on a small number, though. Only 34 chocolate Labs and 139 black or yellow Labs with a known colour had died, so a few dogs either way could move the figure. The authors said they knew of no other breed where coat colour had been linked to lifespan.

They offered a possible reason. Chocolate Labs in the same study had more ear and skin problems: ear inflammation (otitis externa) was recorded in 23.4% of chocolate dogs, against 17.0% of yellow and 12.8% of black. The authors suggested that a heavier lifetime load of such disease might affect how long a dog lives. That is their idea to test, not a proven cause.

How long do labs live, male vs female?

The 2018 study did not split the age at death by sex. Across all dogs in the 2024 study, females lived a little longer, with a median of 12.7 years against 12.4 for males.

The Lab-specific figures we found come from a 2019 study of neutered pet dogs at about 900 North American vet hospitals. It only took dogs that were between 6.5 and 8.5 years old and still under care at 9.5, so its medians run higher than studies that count every dog from birth:

Labradors in the 2019 study Male Female
Normal body condition 13.3 years 13.6 years
Overweight 12.7 years 13.0 years

Females had a small edge, and weight made a bigger difference than sex.

What do labs usually die of?

In the 2018 VetCompass study, 147 deaths had a recorded cause. The most common were:

  • musculoskeletal disorders (joints, bones, muscles), 24.5%, at a median age of 13.4 years
  • cancer, 21.1%, at a median age of 10.6 years
  • mass lesions, 7.5%
  • brain, liver and kidney disorders, 5.4% each

The authors pointed out that cancer had the bigger effect on lifespan of the two, while joint and muscle problems tended to end life later. Among living Labs, the most common problems recorded in a year were ear inflammation (10.4%), overweight or obesity (8.8%) and degenerative joint disease (5.5%). None of these is something to read off a chart for your own dog. If you notice stiffness, a lump or a change in appetite, mention it to your vet.

Does weight change how long a lab lives?

It does. A lifelong feeding study of 48 Labrador Retrievers, published in 2002, is the clearest evidence we found. The dogs were paired and one dog in each pair was fed 25% less than its pair-mate from 8 weeks old. The leaner dogs had a significantly longer median lifespan, and signs of chronic disease generally appeared later.

The 2019 study found the same in ordinary homes. Overweight Labs of both sexes had median lifespans 0.6 years shorter than Labs of normal body condition (see the table above). Labs in the 2018 UK study weighed 33 kg on average, so a few kilograms of extra weight is a noticeable share of the dog. Your vet can tell you whether your Lab is carrying extra.

Do labs live longer in other countries?

Some figures say yes, one says no. The 2022 UK life-table paper compared its own result with published figures elsewhere: Labradors lived a mean of 14.1 years in Japan, against a life expectancy at birth of 11.77 in the UK, and a median of 10.5 years was reported in Denmark. The authors noted that the Japanese tables came from a smaller sample, and that different data sources might explain part of the gap. The numbers are not a straight comparison.

When is a lab considered a senior?

At about 9.8, by the vets' definition. The 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines say the senior stage starts in the last 25% of the estimated lifespan, and 75% of the UK median of 13.1 years is 9.8. Our senior dog age chart by breed does the same sum for 36 breeds.

VCA Animal Hospitals uses a simpler weight guide and puts dogs of 51 to 100 lb at senior from about 6 to 7, so the two methods give quite different answers for a Lab. VCA's advice for senior dogs:

  1. See the vet every six months instead of once a year.
  2. Ask your vet to check for arthritis pain and whether a senior diet would help.
  3. Keep up regular exercise, but stop when your dog starts hanging back on walks or loses interest in a game.
  4. Give soft places to sleep, and put rugs or carpet on slippery floors.
  5. Keep the mind busy with new toys and tricks.

Questions people ask

How long do labs live on average?

About 12 to 13 years. A 2024 Dogs Trust study of 43,428 UK Labradors found a median lifespan of 13.1 years. UK vet records give a median of 12.0 years (2013 data) and a life expectancy at birth of 11.77 years (2016 to 2020). The AKC lists 11 to 13 years.

Do chocolate labs live shorter lives?

In one UK study, yes: chocolate Labradors that died had a median age of 10.7 years, against 12.1 for black and yellow Labs. The chocolate figure comes from just 34 dogs, so treat it as a signal rather than a settled number. Chocolate Labs in the same study also had more ear and skin disease.

Do female labs live longer than males?

Slightly, in a 2019 North American study of neutered Labs that reached middle age: normal-weight females had a median of 13.6 years and males 13.3. Across all breeds in a 2024 UK study, females lived a median 12.7 years and males 12.4.

What do labs usually die of?

In a VetCompass study of UK Labs, the most common recorded causes of death were musculoskeletal disorders (24.5%) and cancer (21.1%). Labs that died of joint and muscle problems had a median age at death of 13.4 years; those that died of cancer, 10.6 years.

At what age is a lab a senior?

At about 9.8 years, using the AAHA definition of senior as the last 25% of expected lifespan and the UK median of 13.1. VCA's weight guide puts dogs of 51 to 100 lb at about 6 to 7. Your vet can say where your own Lab sits.
