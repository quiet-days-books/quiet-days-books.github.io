In short

  • A dog memory book works best filled now, while you can still check the details against the dog in front of you.
  • Give it sections: your story, photos, keepsakes, sounds and video, places, and other people's memories.
  • Choose 25 photos by category instead of scrolling for favorites.
  • Write dates on everything, and add their weight and height at the shoulder: photos have no scale.
  • Use photo-safe albums, no sticky pages or tape, and keep digital copies in more than one place.

A good dog memory book holds the things you are most likely to forget: the day you met, every nickname, their habits, about 25 chosen photos, a dated paw print with their measurements, a little shed fur, their sounds, and a line from each person they love. Fill it while your dog is still here, so you can check the details and take what is missing.

What to put in a dog memory book

Most memory books for sale give you a page for the name, the birthday and a favorite toy, then lots of photo frames. That is a start. A book you will actually reread has more of the everyday in it.

Ideas for the story pages

The story fades before the photos do. The date goes first, then the weather, then the order things happened in. Write these down as scenes, not summaries:

  • The day you met: where you were, who was with you, what they were doing when you first saw them, whether they came to you. If you don't know the exact date, write the month and year and say so.
  • The first day home: where they went first. Where they chose to sleep, versus where you set up the bed.
  • Every nickname, including the ones that have fallen out of use. Ask everyone in the house; someone will have one you never heard.
  • The funniest thing they ever did, written the way you tell it at dinner, plus two smaller stories you never tell.
  • The biggest trouble they caused: the shoes, the cake, the escape. Include what it cost.
  • What they taught you. Finish "Because of them, I now..." with something only true of your dog.

A page of plain habits belongs here too: what time they wake you, the order of their evening, the spot where they always stop on the walk.

Dog photo book ideas: choose 25

A phone with three thousand photos of your dog is a pile, and nobody looks through a pile. Our book suggests picking 25 by category, so you choose instead of scrolling:

  1. Five from the earliest days you have.
  2. Five of the ordinary: the sofa, the kitchen, the back seat, mid-walk, asleep.
  3. Three with other people in them.
  4. Three from places that matter.
  5. Three of them doing the thing they most obviously love.
  6. Three that are technically bad photos you can't delete.
  7. Two with both of you in the frame.
  8. One that is somehow the most them.

When two photos are nearly the same, keep the one with more of the room in it. Background is what tells you later which house and which year it was. If you are missing some of these, our senior dog photoshoot ideas are planned around what an old dog can comfortably do.

Keepsake pages: paw print, fur and collar

Keepsakes are where a memory book becomes an object. A few that suit a living dog:

  • Paw print. Use a kit made for pets, ink-free paper or a soft clay tray, and follow its instructions. Do it while they are relaxed and stop if they pull away. Write the date on the back with their weight, height at the shoulder and length nose to tail. A photograph has nothing in it to measure against, so these numbers are often the only record of how big they really were.
  • Shed fur. Keep a pinch from the brush after grooming, in a small envelope or jar, dated. Only naturally shed fur; never cut it for this. A second sample a few years later shows how the coat changed.
  • Collar and tag. Photograph them flat in daylight, the tag close enough to read. Keep the old collars when they are replaced; lined up, they show every size the dog has been.
  • The favorite toy. Photograph it as it is, damage and all, then with them holding it.

Sounds and video in a paper book

Most memory books leave this part out, yet a paper book can still hold it. Record their footsteps on a hard floor, their breathing while they sleep, the greeting at the door, the sigh when they lie down. Twenty or thirty seconds each on a phone is plenty.

Put the files in one folder, named clearly ("footsteps-kitchen-2026"). In the printed book, add a page that lists each recording with its date, and a QR code or link to the folder if you use cloud storage.

How to make the pages last

A memory book is meant to be opened in twenty years, so the materials matter. The Library of Congress gives clear advice for photographs:

  • Use photo-safe albums and sleeves. Avoid albums with colored, "magnetic" or sticky self-adhesive pages.
  • Don't use tape, glue, sticky notes or other adhesives on the photos themselves. Photo corners or sleeves hold them instead.
  • If you write on a print, use a soft pencil on the back near the edge, or write on the page beside it.
  • Store the book somewhere cool and fairly dry, not in a damp basement or a hot attic.

For digital files, the Library's advice is to make copies and store them in separate places. A phone alone is one lost phone away from nothing. A second copy on a computer or drive, and a third in cloud storage, is a reasonable setup.

Where to start this week

Start small. Write down the day you met, tonight, while the dog is asleep nearby. Then add a letter to your dog as the last page, dated today. Questions people ask

What do you put in a dog memory book?

The day you met and their first day home, every nickname, a page of their habits, about 25 chosen photos, a paw print with the date and their measurements, a small dated envelope of shed fur, a photo of their collar and tag, a list of the places that belong to your story, and a line or two from the people they love. Add links or QR codes to sound and video files if you keep it on paper.

Should I make a dog memory book before my dog dies?

Yes, if you can. You remember more while the dog is here, you can still take the photos and recordings you are missing, and the dog can be part of making it: the paw print, the photo of them with their toy, the walk you film.

Digital or printed memory book for my dog?

Both, ideally. Paper is easy to pick up and show people. Digital holds sound and video. Whichever you choose, keep copies of the files in more than one place.

How many photos should go in a dog memory book?

Fewer than you think. Around 25 chosen by category tells the whole story and can be looked through in a few minutes. A phone full of photos is a pile, not a record.
