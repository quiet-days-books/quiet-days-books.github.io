In short

  • Dogs can eat parsnips: the PDSA lists plain boiled parsnips, without glaze or oil, among foods dogs can share at Christmas.
  • The roast parsnips on the table are the problem: oil, butter, honey or maple glaze, salt and sometimes garlic.
  • PDSA lists carrots boiled or raw but parsnips only boiled; we found no vet source that covers raw parsnip, so cook it.
  • Parsnip leaves, stems and seeds carry furocoumarins, chemicals that make skin burn in sunlight. Keep dogs off parsnip tops and wild parsnip on walks.
  • Feed a small portion for your dog's size, and ask your vet first if your dog is on a special diet.

Yes, dogs can eat parsnips if they're plain and cooked. The PDSA, a UK vet charity, lists plain boiled parsnips, without any glaze or oil, among the Christmas foods dogs can share, in a small portion that suits your dog's size. The roast parsnips on the table are a different food: oil, butter, honey or maple glaze and seasoning make them a poor choice for a dog.

The ASPCA's list of foods to avoid doesn't name parsnips. The real questions are about the version on the plate, raw parsnip, and the plant the root grows from, which is where a dog can get into trouble on a walk.

Are parsnips safe for dogs?

The root is. The PDSA's guide to a pet-friendly Christmas puts "plain boiled parsnips (without any glaze or oil)" on its list of foods to share, next to plain carrots, carrot and swede mash and lean white turkey meat. A PDSA press release from December 2022 lists parsnips among the vegetables dogs can have, with carrots, green beans, Brussels sprouts, broccoli, peas and cauliflower.

PDSA vet Lynne James adds the condition that matters most: the food should be plain, "not covered in glaze, sauce, gravy, or seasoning," because those can upset a dog's stomach.

Plenty of pages online also list parsnip nutrients. We left those out: we found no vet source that gives them for dogs. Treat parsnip as an extra your dog may enjoy.

Roast parsnips, honey glaze and the Christmas plate

At Christmas parsnips are usually roasted, in oil or goose fat, often with honey or maple syrup, salt, and sometimes garlic or herbs. Each of those is a reason to keep the roast ones on your plate.

The easiest way around this is to cut a few extra pieces before anything goes on them. Boil those in plain water while the rest roast, let them cool, and you have something for your dog's bowl when everyone else sits down.

Can dogs eat raw parsnips?

This is where pages online disagree, and we couldn't settle it from a vet source. What we can point to is the PDSA list itself. For carrots it says "plain boiled or raw." For parsnips it says only "plain boiled." Since that's the only charity guidance we found that names parsnips, cooking them is the option backed by a source.

Boil the pieces until soft and cut them small.

How much parsnip can a dog have?

The PDSA doesn't give a figure for parsnips. Its guidance is to feed a small portion appropriate for your dog's size and ideal weight, and to cut back their normal food to match. It also says a pet-size portion is much smaller than ours, even for a large breed. The AKC's Thanksgiving guide doesn't mention parsnips, but its list of foods dogs can share talks about small amounts of plain vegetables too. A few cooked pieces mixed into the usual dinner fits both.

Keep the first portion small. If your dog is on a diet chosen by your vet, for weight, kidneys, blood sugar or anything else, ask before adding parsnip or any other extra.

Parsnip leaves, garden plants and wild parsnip on walks

This part of the question rarely gets asked. The parsnip you buy and the plant it grows from are not the same risk.

NC State Extension treats garden parsnip and wild parsnip as the same species: modern varieties were bred from the wild plant. It says all parts of the plant contain furocoumarins, which make skin sensitive to light and can lead to severe burns and blisters, lists the flowers, fruits, leaves, sap, seeds and stems as the poisonous parts, and names dogs among the groups at risk. It also says that only the first-year root is edible.

The University of Maine Extension describes how the burn happens: sap gets on the skin, the skin is then exposed to sunlight, and the result can be severe blistering. Wild parsnip grows on roadsides and field edges that aren't mowed often. In its second year it reaches about 3 to 5 feet and carries umbel-shaped clusters of yellow flowers.

A related plant, cow parsnip (also listed under the name giant hogweed), is on the ASPCA's list as toxic to dogs, cats and horses, with photosensitization (sunburn, dermatitis) as the sign.

In practice:

  • If you grow parsnips, don't let your dog chew the tops or play in the bed once the plants are tall or flowering.
  • On walks, keep your dog out of tall stands of yellow-flowered plants along verges and field edges in summer.
  • If your dog has been in them and later has red, sore or blistered skin, especially where the fur is thin, call your vet. The ASPCA Poison Control line is (888) 426-4435; it says a consultation fee may apply.

Parsnips for an older dog

Many older dogs are on a set diet, and a holiday meal is where it's most likely to slip. A plain boiled parsnip is a gentle way to let an older dog share the day, as long as the vet's plan allows it.

Our book One Day, You'll Miss This has a page called "Have a Picnic Together." It starts from the observation that you and your dog have "shared a home for years and almost never a meal," and asks you to bring something for them from home, in their own bag. A few plain parsnip pieces packed for a winter picnic, eaten side by side, fit that page well. The same page warns that a picnic basket holds more things a dog shouldn't have than any other meal, so keep your own food to yourself.

Questions people ask

Can dogs eat parsnips?

Yes. The PDSA lists plain boiled parsnips, without any glaze or oil, among the Christmas foods dogs can have, in a small portion suited to their size and ideal weight.

Can dogs eat roast parsnips?

Not the ones roasted for people. PDSA vet Lynne James says shared food should be plain and not covered in glaze, sauce, gravy or seasoning, as these could upset a dog's stomach. Set a few pieces aside before the oil and honey go on and boil them instead.

Can dogs eat raw parsnips?

We couldn't find a vet or official source that covers raw parsnip. The PDSA allows carrots boiled or raw but lists parsnips only as plain boiled, so cooking them is the choice backed by a source.

Can dogs eat parsnip peel or tops?

No source we read covers peel; if you're unsure, peel and boil it. The leaves, stems and seeds are different: NC State Extension says those parts contain furocoumarins, which can cause burns and blisters when skin meets sunlight, and only the first-year root is edible.

Is wild parsnip dangerous to dogs?

It can be. NC State Extension lists dogs among the groups at risk from wild parsnip, whose sap makes skin sensitive to sunlight. If your dog has been rolling or chewing in tall yellow-flowered plants on a roadside and the skin looks red or sore later, call your vet.
