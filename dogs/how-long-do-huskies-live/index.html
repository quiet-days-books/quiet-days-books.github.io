Siberian Huskies usually live about 12 years. The largest study we found that counts them, a 2024 Dogs Trust analysis of UK records that included 4,453 Huskies, found a median lifespan of 11.9 years. That is close to the average dog, a little under it, and below the 12 to 14 years the American Kennel Club lists for the breed.

The Dogs Trust figure is the one to lean on. It comes from the most dogs, and it is a median: half the Huskies had died by 11.9 and half lived longer. The owner survey runs higher and the life table runs lower, for reasons explained below. So "about 12" is the best short answer. The owner survey's median sits a little higher, at 12 years 7 months.

Do Huskies live longer than other dogs their size?

You will read that they do. The data we found says otherwise.

The 2024 Dogs Trust study put the Siberian Husky in its medium size group. Medium purebreds there had a median lifespan of 12.5 years, the same as all dogs together. Crossbreeds had 12.0. Huskies came in at 11.9, and their hazard ratio against crossbreeds was 1.15: at any given age, a Husky in the data was about 15% more likely to die than a crossbreed of the same age.

Why do the Husky numbers disagree?

Because each source measures something different.

The Kennel Club survey of 2004 asked owners to report on their dogs. Owners who fill in a health survey may not be a typical slice of Husky owners, and only 129 deaths were reported. The 2014 repeat of the survey collected just 15 Husky deaths, and the breed health plan gives only their age range, 3 to 15 years.

The 2022 life tables from the Royal Veterinary College count something else again. Their "life expectancy at birth" is an average, and an average is pulled down by dogs that die young. Huskies in that study had a higher chance of dying before age 2 than dogs overall. The study had only 153 Huskies, and its authors list the Husky among the breeds whose sample was small, so the table needs careful reading. Part of the gap is method rather than dogs: all dogs together came out at 11.23 years in the life tables, against 12.5 in the 2024 study.

The AKC range comes with no study or method cited on its breed page. And we looked for a dedicated VetCompass study of Siberian Huskies, the kind that exists for Shih Tzus and Beagles. The Kennel Club's breed health plan for Huskies (May 2021) says none had been published, and we did not find one either.

What do Huskies usually die of?

The only Husky-specific figures we found come from the 2004 owner survey, summarised in the Kennel Club's breed health plan. Of 129 deaths:

  • cancer: 41 dogs (31.8%)
  • old age: 21 dogs (16.3%)
  • neurological causes: 9 dogs (7.0%)
  • heart: 8 dogs (6.2%)
  • gut: 7 dogs (5.4%)

The youngest of those dogs died at 5 months and the oldest at 18 years and 1 month.

Asked to rank the breed's most important health concerns in 2018 and 2019, the Husky breed health coordinator named glaucoma first, then hereditary cataract, then epilepsy. Eye problems also led the survey of living dogs: of 797 Huskies reported in 2004, 29% had at least one condition, and the largest group of those conditions was eye-related. A 2021 study of dogs seen with cataracts, summarised in the plan, found that the Huskies were a mean 3.5 years old when they came in, against 9.5 for the other dogs.

None of this predicts what will happen to your own dog. If you are choosing a puppy, ask the breeder about eye screening of the parents, which the breed club encourages for all breeding dogs, and talk to your vet about what to watch for as your Husky ages.

Do male or female Huskies live longer?

We found no Husky figures by sex. Across all breeds in the 2024 Dogs Trust study, females had a median of 12.7 years and males 12.4, so if Huskies follow the general pattern the difference would be a few months. The 2022 life tables found the same direction for dogs overall: 11.41 years for females, 11.07 for males.

When does a Husky become a senior?

At about 9. The American Animal Hospital Association defines the senior stage as the last 25% of a dog's estimated lifespan. Using the 2024 median of 11.9 years, 75% is about 8.9; using the owner survey's 12 years 7 months, it is about 9.4.

VCA Animal Hospitals uses weight bands instead: about 7 to 9 years for dogs of 21 to 50 lb, and about 6 to 7 for dogs of 51 to 100 lb. The AKC gives Huskies 35 to 50 lb for females and 45 to 60 lb for males, so a female falls in the first band and a large male in the second. That is earlier than the 25% rule; both are rough guides.

Our senior dog age chart by breed sets the two methods side by side, and the dog age calculator turns your Husky's age into human years.

How to help a Husky live longer

Most of this is the same as for any older dog, from VCA's advice for seniors and from the weight study below.

  1. Keep your Husky lean. A 2019 study of North American vet records looked at 12 breeds. In every one, neutered dogs that were overweight at ages 6.5 to 8.5 had a shorter median lifespan than dogs in normal condition. Huskies were not among the 12, so we cannot give a Husky number, but the pattern held across sizes. Ask your vet whether your dog is carrying extra.
  2. From around 9, book vet checks every six months instead of once a year, as VCA recommends for senior dogs.
  3. Keep up daily exercise, and ease off when your dog starts lagging on walks or losing interest in play. Our guide to how much to walk a senior dog covers distance and pace.
  4. Put down soft bedding and rugs on slippery floors.
  5. Keep the mind busy with new toys and training.

An older Husky may still want to be out in the cold. Our page on whether dogs get cold covers when an older dog needs help with the weather, and the senior dog bucket list has ideas sorted by energy level.

Questions people ask

What is the average lifespan of a Siberian Husky?

About 12 years. A 2024 Dogs Trust study of 584,734 UK dogs, including 4,453 Siberian Huskies, found a median lifespan of 11.9 years for the breed. The Kennel Club's 2004 owner survey gave 12 years 7 months from 129 deaths, and the American Kennel Club lists 12 to 14 years.

Do Huskies live longer than other dogs their size?

Not in the largest study. In the 2024 Dogs Trust data, medium-sized purebreds had a median of 12.5 years and crossbreeds 12.0, while Siberian Huskies had 11.9. Their risk of death at any age was 15% higher than that of crossbreeds.

Can a Husky live to 15?

Some do. In the Kennel Club's 2004 survey, the oldest of 129 Huskies reported by owners died at 18 years and 1 month, and the 2014 survey recorded deaths up to 15 years. Half of all Huskies in the 2024 UK study had died by 11.9, so 15 is well above typical.

What do Huskies usually die of?

In the Kennel Club's 2004 survey, owners reported cancer in 31.8% of 129 deaths, old age in 16.3%, neurological causes in 7.0%, heart problems in 6.2% and gut problems in 5.4%. That survey is small and owner-reported, so treat it as a rough picture.

At what age is a Husky a senior?

At about 9. The American Animal Hospital Association puts the senior stage in the last 25% of a dog's expected lifespan, and 75% of 11.9 years is about 8.9. VCA's weight guide puts dogs of 21 to 50 lb at about 7 to 9 and dogs of 51 to 100 lb at about 6 to 7. Ask your vet where your own Husky sits.
