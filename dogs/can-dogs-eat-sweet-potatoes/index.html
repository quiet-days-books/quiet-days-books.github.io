In short

  • Dogs can eat sweet potato when it's fully cooked, peeled and plain, as an occasional extra in small amounts.
  • Raw sweet potato is hard to chew and digest, and raw pieces or skin can cause choking or a blockage.
  • Holiday versions are a different food: casserole with butter, brown sugar or marshmallows, pie and seasoned fries are not for dogs.
  • Treats, sweet potato included, should stay under 10% of daily calories, says PetMD.
  • Diabetic, overweight or older dogs on a set diet: ask your vet first. Even the big sources disagree on how sweet potato affects blood sugar.

Yes, dogs can eat sweet potatoes when they are fully cooked, peeled and plain, and given in small amounts as an extra. Raw sweet potato, the skin, and holiday dishes made with butter, brown sugar, marshmallows or seasoning are the parts to skip. If your dog is diabetic, overweight or on a diet your vet chose, ask before you add any.

That's the short version, and almost every source agrees on it. Where they stop agreeing is blood sugar and a heart-disease question that the AKC now mentions at the top of its page. Both are below.

Are sweet potatoes good for dogs?

They can be a decent extra. PetMD lists fiber, beta-carotene (which the body turns into vitamin A), vitamin C, manganese, potassium and iron. The AKC's page adds that sweet potato is low in fat and a common carbohydrate in commercial dog foods.

"Good for dogs" has limits, though. The AKC says too much vitamin A can cause bone and muscle weakness, and that carbohydrates like sweet potato shouldn't be a dog's main source of nutrition: animal protein belongs in the daily diet. A dog on a complete, balanced food already gets what it needs from the bowl. Sweet potato is something you add because your dog likes it, not something the bowl is missing.

Raw, cooked or mashed: how to serve it

Cook it until it's soft. The AKC says never to feed raw sweet potato: it's hard to chew, can upset the stomach and can cause an intestinal blockage. PetMD gives the same reason for raw pieces, plus a choking risk.

Peel it, too. PetMD says the skin isn't toxic but is hard to digest and can cause choking or a blockage.

A simple way to do it:

  1. Peel the sweet potato and cut it into chunks.
  2. Boil, steam or bake it plain, with no butter, oil, salt or spice.
  3. Mash it or cut it small, especially for a dog who gulps.
  4. Let it cool, then mix a spoonful into the usual food.
  5. Start with a small amount the first time and watch how your dog's stomach handles it. The AKC suggests checking with your vet before adding a new food.

How much sweet potato can a dog have?

PetMD keeps every treat, sweet potato included, under 10% of daily calories, with the other 90% coming from a complete and balanced diet. Its suggested servings, two to three times a week:

Dog's weight Serving (PetMD)
2-10 lb 1 tablespoon
11-20 lb 1-2 tablespoons
21-50 lb 2-3 tablespoons
51-90 lb 3 tablespoons
91 lb and up 4 tablespoons

Too much shows up in the gut first. PetMD lists gas, bloating, vomiting and diarrhea, especially after a big portion or raw sweet potato. Your vet can change these amounts for your dog.

Sweet potato casserole, pie and fries

Most of the sweet potato a dog meets in November and December has been turned into something else. The AKC's Thanksgiving guide says plain mashed sweet potato is generally safe, and tells owners to avoid versions topped with brown sugar, marshmallows, maple syrup or other sugary ingredients. The same guide lists casseroles and "cookies, pies, and sweets" among foods not to feed.

If your dog got into a sugar-free sweet potato pie or dessert, check the label for xylitol. The ASPCA says desserts with artificial sweeteners like xylitol can cause low blood sugar, liver damage or even death. Call your vet, or Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661 (its site lists a $99 incident fee). Pie spice is its own question, covered on our page about whether dogs can eat pumpkin pie.

Sweet potatoes and blood sugar: the sources disagree

This is the part that confuses people, and with reason. PetMD, reviewed by a vet, says sweet potatoes have a relatively low glycemic index. The AKC quotes a vet who says the opposite: sweet potatoes have a high glycemic index and should be added to a diabetic dog's diet with caution.

You don't need to settle that argument. Both pages land on the same advice. PetMD says diabetic dogs should only eat sweet potato if their vet recommends it as part of their diet, and the AKC says diabetic, overweight or less active dogs should get only a minimal amount. If your dog is in any of those groups, the decision is your vet's.

What the AKC's "may not be appropriate" note means

The AKC's page now opens with an update: recent research suggests sweet potatoes, while not poisonous, may not be appropriate for a dog's diet. The note points to the FDA's investigation into certain dog foods and dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM), a disease of the heart muscle.

The FDA page gives the numbers. Among diets in DCM cases reported between January 2014 and April 2019, 93% contained peas and/or lentils and 42% contained potatoes or sweet potatoes. The FDA wrote that the reports alone do not supply enough data to establish a cause, called the link a complex question that may involve several factors, and said in its last update, in December 2022, that it would not publish more until there is meaningful new science.

Those numbers are about foods where potatoes, peas or lentils are main ingredients, not about a spoonful on top of dinner. If you have questions about your dog's food, the FDA's advice is to work with your vet before changing it. It also says to call your vet soon if your dog shows decreased energy, a cough, trouble breathing or collapses.

Sweet potato for an older dog

Older dogs are often the ones on a special diet, and the holidays are when that diet slips. If your vet has chosen a food for kidneys, weight or blood sugar, a new extra should go past them first, even a plain one.

Otherwise, a spoonful of plain sweet potato is one of the easier ways to give a dog a share of the holiday meal. The ASPCA suggests mixing a few pieces of turkey, sweet potato and green beans into their usual dinner. Our book One Day, You'll Miss This has a page called "Capture Meal Time" that asks you to film the whole ritual, starting before the cupboard opens, because "everything good happens before the food does." A holiday dinner is a good evening to try it.

Questions people ask

Can dogs eat sweet potatoes?

Yes. PetMD says dogs can eat sweet potatoes as long as they are fully cooked, plain and given in moderation. Peel them, skip butter, sugar and seasoning, and treat them as an extra rather than a meal.

Can dogs eat raw sweet potato?

No. The AKC says raw sweet potato is hard to chew, can upset the stomach and can cause an intestinal blockage. Cook it until soft.

Can dogs eat sweet potato skin?

It's better to peel it. PetMD says the skin isn't toxic but is hard to digest and can be a choking or blockage risk.

How much sweet potato can I give my dog?

PetMD keeps all treats under 10% of daily calories and suggests about 1 tablespoon for a 2-10 lb dog, 2-3 tablespoons for 21-50 lb and 4 tablespoons for dogs over 90 lb, two to three times a week. Your vet can adjust this.

Can dogs eat sweet potato casserole?

No. The AKC says plain mashed sweet potato is generally safe but to avoid versions topped with brown sugar, marshmallows, maple syrup or other sugary ingredients, and it lists casseroles among foods not to feed.

Are sweet potatoes linked to heart disease in dogs?

Not proven. The FDA looked into dog foods and a heart condition called DCM. Potatoes or sweet potatoes were main ingredients in 42% of reported diets, peas or lentils in 93%, and the FDA said the reports don't establish cause. A spoonful of sweet potato as a treat is a different thing from a food built on it; ask your vet about your dog's diet.
