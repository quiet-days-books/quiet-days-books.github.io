In short

  • No study has measured how many years a dog remembers a person. What has been measured points to long memory for familiar people.
  • Dogs recalled what a person did after 1 hour without expecting a test, and imitated actions as well after 24 hours as straight away (Fugazza and colleagues, 2016).
  • Dogs that learn toy names kept most of them for at least two months without practice (Dror and colleagues, 2021).
  • A familiar person's scent lit up a reward area of the dog brain more than any other scent tested, and hearing the owner's voice calls up the owner's face.
  • The 'two minutes' short-term memory figure comes from tests of arbitrary signs in 25 species, not from tests of dogs remembering people.

Dogs remember the people they live with for the long term, but nobody has measured exactly how long. Studies show dogs recalling a person's actions after a day, keeping toy names for at least two months, and responding most strongly to the scent of a familiar person. No published study we found has tested whether a dog knows someone after years apart.

So the honest answer has two parts: what has been measured, and what owners report but science has not checked.

How long do dogs remember you?

As far as research can say, for a long time, and through more than one sense. Dogs know familiar people by scent, by voice and by face, and their memory for what a person did has been tested over a full day. The longest measured memory we found is two months, and that study stopped there, not the dogs.

None of these studies followed dogs for years. That gap is the reason websites disagree so much.

How long is a dog's short-term memory?

Short, for arbitrary things, and that is where the popular "two minutes" figure comes from. A 2015 review pooled more than 90 sets of memory tests on 25 species, in which an animal sees a sign and must pick it out again after a delay. Across those species, spans ran from a few seconds to several minutes.

We could not find a dog-specific two-minute result in that review. Its authors also note that some animals remember certain things for days or months, such as where they hid food, and explain this by memory systems built for information that matters to the animal.

A random shape on a screen is not you. The review does not test people, but the studies below do, and they find memories lasting hours, a day and months.

Do dogs remember what you did?

Yes, at least for an hour, even when they were not trying to. In a 2016 study in Current Biology, a team in Budapest first trained 17 dogs to copy human actions on the cue "Do it". Then they retrained the dogs to simply lie down after watching a person do something, so the dogs stopped expecting to copy.

When the researchers then said "Do it" by surprise, the dogs could still reproduce what they had seen after 1 minute and after 1 hour. Their recall faded more over time than when they had expected to imitate. The authors call this episodic-like memory: remembering an event nobody asked you to remember.

In an earlier study, the same team tested 12 pet dogs on imitating an action after delays of 1 to 24 hours. Performance stayed consistently high however long the wait.

How do dogs remember people?

By scent first, and also by voice and face. In a 2015 study, 12 dogs trained to lie still in an MRI scanner smelled five scents: their own, a familiar person, a stranger, a familiar dog and a strange dog. The caudate, a brain area linked to positive expectation, responded most to the familiar person.

That familiar person was not even in the room. The scent alone was enough.

Voice works too. In a 2007 study in Animal Cognition, dogs heard either their owner's voice or a stranger's, then saw a face on a screen. They looked longer when the face did not match the voice, which suggests the owner's voice calls up a picture of the owner's face.

Faces on their own are harder. In a 2013 study in Vienna, 15 dogs learned to choose between the heads of two familiar people. Performance dropped when real heads were swapped for photos, and dogs varied a lot, but two dogs mastered every stage, so dogs can in principle tell familiar people apart by face alone.

Do dogs remember people after years?

Nobody has tested it. We found no published study that separated dogs from a person for years and then measured recognition. Reunion videos and owners' stories say yes, often, but they are not measurements, and they rarely show the dogs who did not react.

What can be said is that the measured memories are long relative to the tests. In a 2021 study, six "gifted word learner" dogs learned the names of new toys in a week and most still knew them after two months, with no practice in between. A person a dog lived with every day for years is learned far more deeply than a toy's name, by scent, voice, face and routine.

The same applies to previous owners. A rehomed dog may well keep the scent and voice of the people it grew up with, but what it remembers of them years later has not been studied.

Do dogs remember you when you are gone?

They register how long you were away. In a 2011 Swedish study, Rehn and Keeling filmed 12 dogs left alone at home for half an hour, 2 hours and 4 hours. At the reunion, dogs left for 2 or 4 hours were more active, wagged their tails more and made more contact with their owners than after half an hour.

While alone, the dogs behaved much the same whatever the length of the absence. The authors could not tell whether the dogs knew how long it had been or only registered it once the owner walked in. Either way, the greeting carries a record of the absence.

That greeting is worth keeping. Our book One Day, You'll Miss This has a page called "Record Their Greeting": next time you come home, film it from your side, phone already recording as the door opens. It also suggests doing it again some other week, because the greeting after a full day away is not the greeting after a ten-minute errand.

Does a dog's memory change with age?

It can, and it is easy to miss. In a 2010 survey of 497 dogs aged 8 to nearly 20, Salvin and colleagues estimated from owners' answers that 14.2% showed signs of cognitive dysfunction, a condition of older dogs that affects memory and behaviour. Only 1.9% had been identified by a vet. The share rose steeply with age.

Signs owners report include seeming lost in a familiar place, sleeping differently, changes with familiar people and forgetting house habits. Many overlap with other health problems, so they are a reason to see your vet, not something to judge at home.

How to give your dog memories worth keeping

Build good associations on purpose. So much of a dog's memory runs on links between a cue and what follows that you can choose some of the links: a turn on the walk that means it is not over yet, a place that means something good happens there.

Two activities from our book work this way:

  • "Take the Long Way Home". When you are heading back, add fifteen minutes on purpose, around the block or the long side of the park. Do it often enough and your dog learns the turn that means the walk is not over.
  • "Go Back Somewhere That Matters". Take them to one of the places that belong to the two of you, the old park or the street from your first year together, and watch what they do when they get there.

You cannot ask whether they remember it. You can watch how they move once they arrive. For more ideas sized to an older dog's energy, our senior dog bucket list sorts them into good days, slow days and days at home, and our list of fun things to do with your senior dog has gentler games.

Your dog will never tell you about the field where they first ran flat out, so that side of the memory is yours to hold. Our dog memory book ideas and the guide to writing a letter to your dog are good places to start, on an ordinary day, with the dog asleep across the room.

Questions people ask

How long do dogs remember you for?

Nobody has put a number on it. Studies have shown dogs recalling a person's actions after 24 hours, remembering words for at least two months, and responding most strongly to the scent of a familiar person. No published study we found has tested recognition after years apart, so stories of reunions after many years are anecdotes, not measurements.

Do dogs remember their previous owners?

Probably, if they lived with them long enough to learn their scent, voice and face, but this has not been tested directly. Research shows dogs know familiar people by scent, voice and face. What a rehomed dog remembers of an earlier owner after a long gap has not been studied.

Do dogs remember people after years?

Owners often say so, but no study we could find has tested it. The longest measured memory we found is two months, for toy names learned in one week. Memory for a person a dog lived with for years is likely stronger than that, but it is not something researchers have measured.

How long do dogs remember your scent?

There is no measured time limit. What is known is that scent matters: in a 2015 brain-scan study of 12 dogs, the scent of a familiar person activated the caudate, an area tied to positive expectation, more than the dog's own scent or the scent of strangers and other dogs.

Do dogs have memories?

Yes. Most of what a dog remembers is by association: a sound, smell or place linked to what usually follows. Studies also show episodic-like memory, where dogs recall a specific thing a person did an hour earlier without having been told to remember it.
