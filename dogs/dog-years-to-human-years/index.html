In short

  • One dog year is not seven human years. The American Veterinary Medical Association, as quoted by the AKC, puts a medium dog's first year at about 15 human years and its second at about 9 more.
  • After age 2 the chart splits by size: at 10 a small dog is about 56 in human years and a giant dog about 79, on the AKC chart.
  • A 2020 study of 104 Labrador Retrievers gives a formula: human age = 16 x ln(dog age) + 31. A 1-year-old comes out at 31, a 12-year-old at about 71.
  • The formula was built on one breed, and its authors say other breeds could age differently.
  • Use the number to judge life stage. For when your dog counts as senior, use the breed-based senior chart instead.

To turn dog years into human years, count the first year as about 15 and the second as about 9 more, so a 2-year-old dog is roughly 24. After that the rate depends on size: small dogs add about 4 human years a year, while giant dogs add 7 or 8 a year from about age 6. The old "multiply by 7" rule is wrong at almost every age.

Is 1 dog year really 7 human years?

No. The American Kennel Club says the 7x rule has been the popular calculation since the 1950s, and that one guess at its origin is that people lived to about 70 and dogs to about 10. Divide one by the other and you get 7.

The trouble is that dogs do not age at an even pace. Every chart and formula below puts a 1-year-old dog somewhere between 12 and 31 in human years, so the rule is far too slow at the start. Later it swings the other way: by 7x, a 14-year-old dog would be 98, while the AKC chart puts a small dog of that age at 72.

The AKC quotes the American Veterinary Medical Association plainly: "dogs do not age at a rate of 7 human years for each year in dog years." The AVMA rule of thumb it gives for a medium-sized dog is:

  1. The first year equals about 15 human years.
  2. The second year adds about 9, so a 2-year-old is about 24.
  3. Each year after that adds roughly 5.

On the AKC's own chart the medium column climbs a little more slowly than that third step, about 4 to 5 years per dog year.

Two things stand out. The methods disagree most about young adults: the formula makes a 3-year-old dog nearly 49, the size chart makes it 28. And they disagree about old giant dogs: the size chart passes 100 at 13, while the formula, built on Labradors, stays in the seventies.

None of these numbers is a measurement of your dog. They are ways of saying "what stage of life is this?" in human terms. To skip the table, our dog age calculator reads it for you, including the months between birthdays.

Dog years to human years for small dogs and big dogs

On the AKC chart, small, medium and large dogs are the same age in human years up to 5. From 6 they split, and the gap widens every year. At 10, a small dog is about 56, a medium dog 60, a large dog 66 and a giant dog 79.

That matches what lifespan studies find. In the largest study so far, of 584,734 dogs in the UK, small purebreds had a median lifespan of 12.7 years, medium 12.5 and large 11.9 (McMillan and colleagues, Scientific Reports, 2024). Larger dogs reach old age sooner, so each year late in life counts for more of their life.

Size is only a rough guide, though. Some small breeds are short-lived and some big ones live long. The same study found a median of 9.8 years for the French Bulldog, a small breed, and 13.2 years for the Golden Retriever, a large one. Our page on how long dogs live has the breed figures.

The dog years to human years formula

In 2020 a team at the University of California San Diego published a different approach in the journal Cell Systems. They compared chemical marks on DNA, called methylation, that build up with age in both species. They sequenced 104 dogs, mostly Labrador Retrievers, aged from 0.1 to 16 years, and matched each dog to the humans whose DNA marks looked most similar.

The result was this formula:

human age = 16 x ln(dog age) + 31

Here ln is the natural logarithm, a key on most phone calculators. To use it:

  1. Type your dog's age in years (for 6 months, type 0.5).
  2. Press ln.
  3. Multiply by 16.
  4. Add 31.

For a 4-year-old dog: ln(4) is about 1.39, times 16 is about 22.2, plus 31 gives about 53.

The curve is steep early and flat late. A 1-year-old is already 31 in human terms, and a 2-year-old about 42, but the step from 12 to 16 adds only about 5 human years.

What the formula gets right, and where it is weak

The authors checked their curve against life stages that both species share. They reported the closest match at the two ends of life. About 8 weeks in a dog lined up with about 9 months in a human, when baby teeth come in for both. At the other end, the 12-year expected lifespan of a Labrador lined up with the worldwide human life expectancy of 70 years.

For the middle years the fit was looser. The paper says that for adolescent and mature dogs the DNA marks changed faster than physiological tables would suggest. That is why the formula makes a 3-year-old dog nearly 49, older than most owners would guess.

The authors also name the main limit themselves: the study "focused exclusively on Labrador retrievers", and breeds with very different lifespans "could yield different age-translation functions." The AKC makes the same point. So the formula is a good fit for Labradors and dogs of similar size. For a Chihuahua or a Great Dane, the size chart is probably the better guide.

How old is a 7 year old dog in human years?

Somewhere between 44 and 62, depending on the method and the dog. Seven is where the methods spread out:

So a 7-year-old Jack Russell is roughly a mid-forties human, and a 7-year-old Great Dane is in its late fifties. For many large breeds, 7 is also close to the age at which vets start to treat a dog as a senior. That depends on the breed's lifespan rather than on a human-years figure, and our senior dog age chart by breed works it out breed by breed.

What a dog's human age is useful for

Mostly for seeing the shape of the years ahead. A 10-year-old Labrador at about 68 in human terms is not middle-aged any more, even if it still runs to the door. A 3-year-old dog at 28 to 49 is a grown adult, not a teenager.

The number does not tell you how long your dog will live or how healthy it is. Your vet can tell you much more from an exam than any chart can. If your dog has moved into the older column of the table, the useful questions are practical ones: how far to walk, what to change at home, and what you want to do together while there is energy for it.

Questions people ask

Is 1 dog year really 7 human years?

No. The 7x rule runs far too slow in the first two years and too fast later. The AVMA, as quoted by the AKC, puts a medium dog's first year at about 15 human years and its second at about 9 more, with roughly 4 or 5 human years a year after that.

What is the formula for dog years to human years?

The best known one comes from a 2020 Cell Systems paper on DNA methylation in Labrador Retrievers: human age = 16 x ln(dog age) + 31, where ln is the natural logarithm. A 2-year-old dog works out at about 42 and a 10-year-old at about 68.

How old is a 7 year old dog in human years?

About 44 for a small dog, 47 for a medium dog, 50 for a large dog and 56 for a giant dog, on the AKC size chart. The Labrador epigenetic formula gives about 62. The old 7x rule says 49, which happens to sit near the middle.

Do small dogs age slower than big dogs in human years?

After the first few years, yes. On the AKC chart, small, medium and large dogs match until age 5, then split. By 10 a small dog is about 56 in human years, a large dog about 66 and a giant dog about 79. Large breeds also tend to have shorter lives.

How do you calculate dog years to human years?

Pick your dog's weight band, then read across the AKC chart at your dog's age. For the epigenetic formula, take the natural logarithm of the age in years, multiply by 16 and add 31. A phone calculator's 'ln' key does the first step.
