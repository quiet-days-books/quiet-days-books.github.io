How the dog name generator works

The list is ours. We wrote it from ordinary words, sorted by something you can see or hear in the dog in front of you: the coat, how big or small they are, how they cross a field, the noise they make at the door, the food they watch you eat, and a set of old-fashioned first names for dogs with a serious face.

The draw is random and runs in your browser. Nothing you choose is saved or sent anywhere. With JavaScript switched off the form does not appear, and the table gives you every name anyway.

It is not a popularity ranking. We have no data on which dog names are common this year, so we do not label any name as popular, and nothing here comes from a registry.

How to choose between the names you get

A dog's name gets shouted across parks, so test it where it will be used.

  • Call each name the way you would across a field. Some look good written down and feel silly at volume.
  • Say it next to the words you already use, such as sit, stay and no, and listen for a clash.
  • Use one for a full day before you decide. By the evening walk you will know.
  • Ask everyone who lives with the dog. A name only one person says turns into a nickname.
  • Look at the dog while you say it. A name taken from something you can see is easier to keep.

If none of the names fits, change the starting point. A grey dog who clears the sofa in one jump may be more of a Rocket than a Pewter.

Naming an adult or an old dog

Not every dog who needs a name is a puppy. A dog adopted at eight may arrive with a name nobody is sure of, or one you would rather not keep.

Dogs Trust teaches a name the same way at any age. Start in a quiet room, with small pieces of a treat your dog really likes, and reward every glance in your direction. When looking at you has become a habit, say the name, and the moment they look, roll a treat out to one side. Repeat, then move to the garden and to busier places, where it is harder for a dog to give you their attention.

Your voice does part of the work. In a 2016 study in Science, Attila Andics and colleagues scanned dogs' brains while the dogs heard praise words and neutral words in praising and neutral tones. The dogs processed the words and the tone separately, and the reward regions became more active only when a praise word came in a praising tone. So say the new name the way you say good things. The names you end up using

The chosen name is only the start. Our book One Day, You'll Miss This has a page called "Write Down Every Nickname". It opens with the line that the name on the paperwork is used roughly four times a year, and asks you to list every variant, including the ones that have fallen out of use, write where each came from if you remember, and ask everyone else in the house what they call the dog.

A new name is a good day to start that list. Questions people ask

How does the dog name generator pick names?

You choose a starting point, such as a brown coat, your dog's size or the sounds they make, and the page draws a few names at random from that part of our list. Press again for a new draw. The draw happens in your browser and nothing is saved or sent.

Are these the most popular dog names?

No. It is our own list of ordinary words that suit a dog, and we have no data on how often any of them is used. It is not taken from a registry or a ranking.

How do I teach my dog a new name?

Dogs Trust's method: in a quiet room, first reward your dog every time they glance at you. Then say the name and, when they look, roll a treat out to one side; repeat, and later practice in the garden and other places with more going on.

Can a dog have more than one name?

Most do. There is the name on the paperwork and the ones used at home: the nickname, the nickname of the nickname, the long formal version for wrongdoing. Use the generator for a second name if your dog already has one, and write down the ones that stick.
