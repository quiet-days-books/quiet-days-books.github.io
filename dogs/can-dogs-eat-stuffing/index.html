In short

  • Dogs shouldn't eat stuffing. The AKC says it commonly contains onions, garlic, butter and seasonings that are unsafe or unhealthy for dogs.
  • Onion, garlic, leeks and chives can damage a dog's red blood cells; Akitas and Shiba Inus are more sensitive than most dogs.
  • Raisins and currants in a stuffing recipe turn it into an urgent call: grapes in any form can injure a dog's kidneys.
  • If your dog ate stuffing, find the recipe, estimate the amount and call your vet or a pet poison line.
  • Instead, set aside plain cooked turkey, green beans or sweet potato for your dog's own bowl before anything is seasoned.

No, dogs shouldn't eat stuffing. Almost every recipe, homemade or boxed, is built on onion and garlic, which can damage a dog's red blood cells, and it is cooked with butter, stock and salt. Many versions add sausage, raisins or nuts, which bring their own risks. The AKC's advice is simple: keep stuffing on human plates.

Stuffing is a firmer no than most of the holiday table. Pumpkin pie is mainly a sugar and fat problem, and gravy depends on what went into it. With stuffing the risky ingredient is usually the base of the dish, not an extra.

Why is stuffing bad for dogs?

The AKC's Thanksgiving guide answers the question directly: stuffing commonly contains onions, garlic, butter and seasonings that can be unsafe or unhealthy for dogs. It puts stuffing on its list of foods not to feed, next to turkey bones and skin, gravy and casseroles.

There are three separate problems in one spoonful:

  • Onion and garlic, the toxic part.
  • Fat from butter, sausage and pan juices, which upsets digestion.
  • Salt from stock cubes and seasoning.

The bread soaks up all three, so there is no plain part to pick out.

Onion and garlic in stuffing

Onion, garlic, chives and leeks all belong to the Allium family. The ASPCA says Allium vegetables can cause stomach irritation and red blood cell damage, which can lead to anemia, and that dogs are at risk depending on the amount eaten. Pet Poison Helpline says all of them are poisonous to dogs, and that some breeds are more sensitive than others, naming Japanese breeds such as the Akita and the Shiba Inu.

That matters for stuffing because the onion is rarely a garnish you can pick out. It is cooked down into the butter and spread through every bite. With a boxed mix, read the ingredient list. The ASPCA's Thanksgiving page makes the same point about the whole meal: onions and garlic are in a lot of Thanksgiving dishes and can be toxic to pets.

What else goes into stuffing?

Stuffing recipes vary more than any other side dish. Here is what common add-ins change, using the sources listed below.

If you don't know the recipe because a guest brought it, assume it has onion and garlic.

My dog ate stuffing: what should I do?

  1. Move the rest out of reach and check that your dog is breathing and acting normally.
  2. Find the recipe or the packet. Look for onion, garlic, raisins, currants, nuts and alcohol.
  3. Estimate how much is gone. The ASPCA asks owners to note the amount eaten before calling.
  4. Call your vet. Out of hours, call ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435 (the ASPCA says a consultation fee may apply) or Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661 (its site lists a $99 per-incident fee, follow-up calls included).
  5. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless your vet or the poison line tells you to. Pet Poison Helpline says not to induce vomiting without consulting a vet or the helpline.
  6. Don't wait to see whether your dog seems fine. The AKC's advice for toxic foods such as onion, garlic, grapes and raisins is to call promptly instead of waiting for symptoms.

Tell the vet your dog's age, weight and any medication. With an older dog, or one on a special diet, that is the information they will want first.

What can my dog have instead of stuffing?

The trick is to set food aside before it is seasoned, not to scrape seasoning off afterwards.

The AKC says dogs can have small amounts of plain turkey, sweet potatoes, potatoes, apples, pumpkin, green beans and peas, prepared without butter, salt or seasonings, and suggests setting aside a plain portion of green beans before toppings go on. The ASPCA suggests a small feast of their own: pieces of boneless, fully cooked turkey, sweet potato and green beans mixed into the dog's usual dinner, or tucked into a puzzle toy so they are busy during your meal.

Remove skin and bones from the turkey. The AKC's chief veterinarian advises against turkey skin, which is high in fat, and cooked bones, which can splinter. If your dog is older, on a prescription diet or has a sensitive stomach, ask your vet before adding anything, plain or not.

Keeping stuffing away from your dog on the day

Most stuffing that dogs eat isn't handed to them. It falls, gets left on a low plate, or goes into the trash with the turkey carcass. A few habits help:

  • Tell guests, and especially children, that the dog doesn't get stuffing. The AKC suggests making sure everyone knows which foods are off-limits before the meal.
  • Keep the serving dish at the back of the counter, not the edge.
  • Bag the carcass and stuffing scraps and take them outside. The ASPCA warns that holiday trash fills with bones, onions and chocolate.
  • Leave a quiet room with a blanket for your dog if the house is full, as the ASPCA suggests.

Questions people ask

Can dogs eat stuffing?

No. The AKC's answer is to keep stuffing on human plates, because it commonly contains onions, garlic, butter and seasonings that can be unsafe or unhealthy for dogs.

My dog ate a little stuffing. What should I do?

Find the recipe or packet and check for onion, garlic, raisins, currants, nuts and sausage. Estimate how much is gone and call your vet. Out of hours, ASPCA Animal Poison Control is at (888) 426-4435 and Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661; both charge a fee. Don't make your dog vomit unless told to.

Is boxed stuffing mix safe for dogs?

Treat it like homemade. Read the ingredient list for onion, garlic, raisins and nuts, and remember it gets made with butter or stock. If any of those are in it, don't share it.

Why are onion and garlic bad for dogs?

They belong to the Allium family with leeks and chives. The ASPCA says Allium vegetables can cause stomach irritation and red blood cell damage, which can lead to anemia, depending on the amount eaten.

What Thanksgiving food can I give my dog instead of stuffing?

The AKC lists small amounts of plain turkey, sweet potatoes, potatoes, apples, pumpkin, green beans and peas, all prepared without butter, salt or seasoning. Set a portion aside before you season, and ask your vet if your dog is on a special diet.
