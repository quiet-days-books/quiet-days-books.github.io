When Is a Dog Considered a Senior? Ages by Size

By Quiet Days Books · Published 30 October 2026 · 5 min read

In short

  • AAHA defines a senior dog as one in the last 25% of its estimated lifespan, through to the end of life. There is no single age.
  • One vet hospital guide puts it at about 9 to 11 for small dogs, 7 to 9 for medium, 6 to 7 for large and 4 to 5 for giant breeds.
  • Applied to measured UK lifespans, the 25% rule gives roughly 10 for a Labrador, 10 and a half for a Miniature Dachshund and 7 and a half for a French Bulldog.
  • A 7-year-old dog may be a senior if it is a large or short-lived breed. Ask your vet to place your own dog.
  • Senior dogs need at least two vet checks a year, easier footing and shorter walks, and they still need fun.

A dog is considered a senior once it enters the last 25% of its expected lifespan. That is the definition in the American Animal Hospital Association's life stage guidelines, and it means there is no single age. For most dogs it falls somewhere between 7 and 10: earlier for giant breeds, later for small dogs that tend to live long.

What age is a senior dog?

Because the definition depends on lifespan, vets usually translate it into rough ages by size. VCA Animal Hospitals, a large network of vet practices, uses these:

Size Weight Senior from about
Small 20 lb or less 9 to 11 years
Medium 21 to 50 lb 7 to 9 years
Large 51 to 100 lb 6 to 7 years
Giant over 100 lb 4 to 5 years

Other guides publish slightly later numbers. PetMD, for example, gives 10 to 12 for small dogs and 6 to 7 for giants. They are all estimates of the same thing. None of them is wrong; they are drawn from different lifespan assumptions, which is why the next section matters more than any one chart.

How vets define a senior dog

The 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines split a dog's life into five stages: puppy, young adult, mature adult, senior and end of life. Senior is defined as "the last 25% of estimated lifespan through end of life." The 2023 AAHA Senior Care Guidelines repeat that definition.

The 2019 guidelines also explain why they do not print an age. Dogs vary more and more as they get older, so the task force chose not to propose age ranges for the mature adult and senior stages. Instead, the vet estimates your dog's likely lifespan, usually from its breed, and works from there.

So the real question behind "when is my dog a senior" is: how long does a dog like mine usually live?

Senior age by breed: doing the math with real lifespans

In 2024, researchers at the charity Dogs Trust published lifespans for 584,734 dogs in the UK. Across all dogs, pure and crossbred, the median lifespan was 12.5 years. Small purebreds had a median of 12.7 years, medium 12.5 and large 11.9.

If you apply the AAHA rule to those medians (senior starts when 75% of the typical lifespan has passed), you get the rough starting points below. We did this arithmetic ourselves; the study does not publish senior ages.

Breed (UK data) Median lifespan Senior from about
Lancashire Heeler 15.4 years 11½
Border Terrier 14.2 years 10½
Miniature Dachshund 14.0 years 10½
Labrador Retriever 13.1 years 10
Border Collie 13.1 years 10
Crossbreeds (all) 12.0 years 9
Pug 11.6 years 8½
French Bulldog 9.8 years 7½
Mastiff 9.0 years 6½ to 7
Cane Corso 8.1 years 6

Read it with three cautions. A median means half of these dogs lived longer, so many dogs are "senior" by this math well before they look it. The data is from UK dogs only. And your own dog's health, weight and history change the picture, which is exactly why the guidelines leave the estimate to your vet.

Is a 7 year old dog a senior?

Sometimes. Seven is the age people ask about most, and the honest answer is that it depends on size and breed.

  • Giant and large breeds: often yes. VCA puts large dogs at 6 to 7 and giants at 4 to 5, and the table above gives about 7 and a half for a French Bulldog and around 6 for a Cane Corso.
  • Medium dogs: possibly. VCA's medium range starts at 7.
  • Small, long-lived dogs: usually not yet. Most will reach senior status somewhere after 9.

Seven is still a sensible age to raise the question with your vet. The RSPCA notes that around seven, many dogs may benefit from gradually moving to a diet made for older dogs. Ask your vet whether that applies to yours before changing food.

Signs your dog is becoming a senior

Age is a number on paper. These are the changes you see at home. The 2019 AAHA guidelines say senior dogs undergo declines in mobility, vision, hearing and cognition. VCA notes how quietly it starts: slightly longer naps and slightly shorter play sessions.

Things owners often notice first:

  • More sleep, and slower to get up after it.
  • Shorter walks, or stopping to rest on the way home.
  • Grey around the muzzle and eyes.
  • Not hearing you come in, or startling when touched while asleep.
  • Hesitating on stairs, slippery floors or jumping into the car.
  • Changes in routine at night, or seeming confused in familiar places.

The AAHA guidelines warn that a senior dog that does not hear people or other pets approaching may snap when startled, so let a sleeping old dog know you are there before you touch them.

None of these signs should be put down to "just old age" without a check. The 2023 AAHA guidelines are blunt about it: "old age is not a disease." Stiffness can be pain, and night-time confusion can be a medical change. Ask your vet about anything new.

What changes when your dog becomes a senior

A few practical things shift once your dog crosses into this stage.

  1. Vet visits. AAHA recommends at least two exams a year for senior dogs, and VCA says every six months instead of once a year.
  2. The home. AAHA suggests traction on floors and stairs, and fewer stairs in the daily routine where possible. A rug on a slippery kitchen floor is a small fix that makes a big difference.
  3. Walks. The RSPCA says senior dogs still need regular exercise, little and often.
  4. Food. Many dogs move to a senior diet, but only on your vet's advice.
  5. Watching. The 2023 AAHA guidelines suggest short videos of your dog walking around the house, because they help your vet spot changes in movement over time.

What does not change: a senior dog still needs a life

Becoming a senior changes the vet schedule and the walks. Most of the rest stays. For a Labrador, the last quarter of a typical lifespan is about three years. That is a long time, and it is the stage where the small daily things count most, because you know they will not last forever.

Most old dogs still want what they always wanted: a sniff around the block at their own pace, a favorite person, a warm spot, a drive with the window open a crack. Questions people ask

What age is a dog considered a senior?

There is no single age. The AAHA guidelines define a senior dog as one in the last 25% of its estimated lifespan. In practice that is often somewhere between 7 and 10, earlier for giant breeds and later for small, long-lived ones.

Is a 7 year old dog a senior?

It depends on size and breed. A 7-year-old Great Dane or French Bulldog is likely a senior; a 7-year-old Chihuahua or Border Terrier usually is not yet. Seven is a good age to ask your vet the question for your own dog.

Is my dog a senior if it still acts young?

Possibly. Senior is a life stage based on expected lifespan, not on how a dog behaves. Many dogs stay lively well into their senior years, and the AAHA guidelines say plainly that old age is not a disease.

How often should a senior dog see the vet?

The AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines recommend at least twice-yearly exams for senior dogs. VCA Animal Hospitals gives the same advice: every six months rather than once a year.

Why do big dogs become seniors sooner?

Because large breeds tend to live shorter lives, so the last quarter of their lifespan starts earlier. In a study of 584,734 UK dogs, small purebreds had a median lifespan of 12.7 years and large purebreds 11.9.
