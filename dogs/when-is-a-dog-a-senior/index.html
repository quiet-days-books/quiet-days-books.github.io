In short

  • Vets call a dog senior in the last 25% of its estimated lifespan (AAHA). There is no single age, so the chart depends on how long the breed usually lives.
  • Applied to median lifespans of 584,734 UK dogs, the rule gives about 11.6 for a Lancashire Heeler, 9.8 for a Labrador, 7.4 for a French Bulldog and 6.1 for a Cane Corso.
  • By weight, VCA Animal Hospitals puts senior at about 9 to 11 for dogs under 20 lb, 7 to 9 for 21 to 50 lb, 6 to 7 for 51 to 100 lb and 4 to 5 over 100 lb.
  • Weight charts miss short-lived small breeds: a French Bulldog or Pug reaches the senior line earlier than its size suggests.
  • AAHA advises at least two vet exams a year for a senior dog. Ask your vet where your own dog sits.

A dog becomes a senior when it enters the last 25% of its expected lifespan. That is the American Animal Hospital Association definition, so the age depends on the breed. Applied to measured UK lifespans, it puts a Lancashire Heeler at about 11.6, a Labrador at about 9.8, a French Bulldog at about 7.4 and a Cane Corso at about 6.

The size classes are the study's own, taken from Kennel Club and FCI breed groups (Supplementary Table 5). The study has no giant class, so a Great Dane or Mastiff counts as large there. The Cane Corso figure rests on only 202 dogs, so treat it as rougher than the Labrador figure, which rests on 43,428.

How is senior age worked out for a breed?

Multiply the breed's median lifespan by 0.75. The 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines define senior as "the last 25% of estimated lifespan through end of life", so the senior line is the point where three quarters of a typical life has passed. For a Labrador: 13.1 × 0.75 = 9.8 years.

The guidelines deliberately do not print ages for the senior stage. Dogs vary more and more as they get older, so the task force left the estimate to the vet, "generally predicted by breed lifespan". The 2023 AAHA Senior Care Guidelines repeat the same definition and say there is no specific age for senior status.

We did the arithmetic ourselves. The study publishes lifespans, not senior ages. You can check any breed it covers the same way: its Supplementary Table 3 lists 155 breeds.

Senior dog age chart by weight

If your dog is a cross or a breed not in the table, weight is the usual shortcut. VCA Animal Hospitals, a large US network of vet practices, uses these bands:

Weight Size Senior from about
20 lb or less Small 9 to 11 years
21 to 50 lb Medium 7 to 9 years
51 to 100 lb Large 6 to 7 years
Over 100 lb Giant 4 to 5 years

The UK study gives a measured version by size class, although its classes come from breed groups, not from weighing dogs. Small purebreds had a median lifespan of 12.7 years, medium 12.5 and large 11.9. By the 25% rule that is about 9.5, 9.4 and 8.9. The gaps between sizes are narrower in the measured data than in the weight chart.

For a crossbreed, the study's median of 12.0 years for all crossbreds gives a senior line of about 9. Your vet can narrow that down from your dog's size and health.

Why the breed chart and the weight chart disagree

Because size predicts lifespan only roughly. Some small breeds live short lives, and a weight chart cannot see that. The French Bulldog is classed as small, so a weight chart says senior at 9 to 11, but its UK median lifespan of 9.8 years puts the 25% line at about 7.4.

The Pug (about 8.7) and the Cavalier King Charles Spaniel and Chihuahua (both about 8.9) also come in under the small-dog band. Going the other way, the Golden Retriever and Labrador are large dogs that live about as long as many small ones in this data.

So if your breed is in the table, use the breed row. Use the weight chart when it is not.

Read either chart with three cautions. A median means half the dogs of that breed lived longer, so plenty of dogs pass the senior line looking young. The data is from UK dogs only. And your dog's own health and history matter more than any average, which is why the guidelines leave the final call to your vet.

Is a 7 year old dog a senior?

For some breeds, yes. By the 25% rule a Cane Corso, Mastiff or St Bernard is a senior by 7, and a French Bulldog or Bulldog is close behind at about 7.4. Most small and medium breeds in the table are not seniors until 9 or later.

The weight chart says much the same. VCA puts large dogs at 6 to 7 and giants at 4 to 5, so a 7-year-old over 50 lb is likely a senior by that measure. A 7-year-old Border Terrier or Jack Russell is still a mature adult, years from the line.

Seven is a sensible age to ask your vet the question for your own dog.

What changes when a dog becomes a senior?

Mostly the vet schedule and the home. The 2019 AAHA guidelines say a senior dog should have at least semiannual exams, and VCA says every six months instead of once a year. The same guidelines describe declines in mobility, vision, hearing and cognition, and suggest small changes at home to keep an older dog safe.

Things to do around the senior line:

  1. Book exams twice a year instead of once.
  2. Add grip to slippery floors and stairs. AAHA suggests traction there, or changing the daily routine so there are fewer stairs.
  3. Let a sleeping old dog know you are there before you touch them. AAHA warns that a senior dog who does not hear people approaching may try to bite when startled.
  4. Mention anything new to your vet: stiffness, sleeping differently, seeming lost in a familiar room. Do not put it down to age without a check.
  5. Ask your vet before changing food or exercise.

VCA adds that the early signs are easy to miss: slightly longer naps, slightly shorter play sessions, grey hairs that hide in most coat colours.

Walks usually change too. Our guide to how much to walk a senior dog helps you judge distance and pace once the senior line is behind you.

How long is the senior stage?

About a quarter of the dog's life, by definition. For a Labrador with a median of 13.1 years that is roughly three years; for a Lancashire Heeler nearly four. Many dogs live past their breed median, so for them the stage is longer still.

That is plenty of time, and it is when the ordinary days start to count. Most old dogs still want what they always wanted: a sniff around the block at their own pace, a favourite person, a warm spot, a drive with the window open a crack. Questions people ask

When is a dog considered a senior?

When it enters the last 25% of its estimated lifespan, according to the 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines. There is no fixed age. For most breeds in UK lifespan data that works out between about 7 and 11, earlier for short-lived breeds and later for long-lived small ones.

Is an 8 year old dog a senior?

Often, for large and short-lived breeds. By the 25% rule a German Shepherd or Boxer turns senior at about 8.5, a Great Dane or Rottweiler at about 8, and a French Bulldog at about 7.4. A Border Terrier or Miniature Dachshund at 8 is still a mature adult.

How many years is considered a senior dog?

It depends on the breed's usual lifespan. Take the breed's median lifespan and multiply by 0.75. Across all 584,734 dogs in a 2024 UK study the median was 12.5 years, which puts the typical senior line at about 9.4 years.

Is there a super senior dog age chart?

Not from a vet body that we could find. The 2019 AAHA guidelines have five stages: puppy, young adult, mature adult, senior and end of life, and end of life is defined by illness, not by age. Some sites use 'super senior' for a dog past its breed's average lifespan, but they do not cite a guideline for it.

How often should a senior dog see the vet?

At least twice a year. The 2019 AAHA guidelines say a senior dog should have at least semiannual exams, and VCA Animal Hospitals gives the same advice: every six months instead of once a year.
