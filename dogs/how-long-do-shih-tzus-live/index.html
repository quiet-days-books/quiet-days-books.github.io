In short

  • Most Shih Tzus live about 12 to 13 years. A 2024 UK study that included 12,007 Shih Tzus found a median lifespan of 12.8 years.
  • That is longer than the 12.5 years for all dogs and well above the 11.2 for flat-faced breeds as a group.
  • UK vet records from 2016 give a median age at death of 12.7 years, with no real difference between males and females.
  • In North American vet records, neutered Shih Tzus of normal weight that were still under care at 9.5 years lived a median of 14.5 years, overweight ones 13.8 to 13.9.
  • By the AAHA rule that the last 25% of expected life is the senior stage, a Shih Tzu is a senior from about 9 to 10.

Shih Tzus usually live about 12 to 13 years. A 2024 Dogs Trust study of UK dogs, which included 12,007 Shih Tzus, found a median lifespan of 12.8 years, a little longer than the average dog. UK vet records give almost the same answer, a median age at death of 12.7 years, and a quarter of those dogs lived past 14.

The first two agree closely, and the Dogs Trust figure rests on by far the most deaths. So 12 to 13 years is the fair answer for a typical Shih Tzu, with many reaching 14 or more. The AKC's range of 10 to 18 years is wide enough to cover nearly every dog.

Why are the Shih Tzu numbers so different?

Because they measure different things.

The life tables give "life expectancy at birth," which is the mean age at death. A mean is pulled down by dogs that die young, so it runs lower than a median. Method matters too: in the same life tables, all UK dogs came out at 11.23 years, against 12.5 in the 2024 Dogs Trust study. On either method, Shih Tzus land close to the average dog or a little above it.

The North American figure runs high for a different reason. The Banfield study only counted dogs seen at 6.5 to 8.5 years that were still under care at 9.5. Every Shih Tzu that died younger was left out, so its medians are higher than a figure counted from birth.

The authors of the UK life tables also noted that a Japanese study built on pet cemetery data put Shih Tzus at 15.0 years. They said the data source may explain part of such gaps: insured dogs may live longer than uninsured ones. So the 15.0 may say more about the data than about Japanese Shih Tzus.

Do Shih Tzus live longer than other flat-faced dogs?

Yes, by a clear margin. Shih Tzus count as a brachycephalic, or flat-faced, breed. In the 2024 Dogs Trust study, flat-faced purebreds as a group had a median lifespan of 11.2 years, against 12.8 for breeds with medium-length faces. Shih Tzus matched the second group, not the first.

The Shih Tzu's hazard ratio against crossbreeds was 0.94, meaning a slightly lower risk of death at any age. The authors of the 2024 VetCompass study reached the same view from their data: Shih Tzus appear relatively long-lived compared with dogs overall.

What health problems do Shih Tzus have?

The 2024 VetCompass study looked at a random 2,423 of the 11,082 Shih Tzus seen by UK vets in 2016. The most common specific problems recorded that year were:

  • gum disease (periodontal disease): 9.5%
  • anal sac impaction: 7.4%
  • ear disorders: 5.5%
  • corneal ulcers: 3.5%

By broad group, skin problems came first (16.6%), then teeth (13.3%) and eyes (11.9%). The authors found Shih Tzus had more anal sac impaction, umbilical hernias and eye problems than earlier reports for dogs overall.

The eyes are the flat-face trade-off. The paper explains that flat-faced dogs have shallow eye sockets, so their eyes stick out more, which makes them prone to problems such as corneal ulcers. Your vet is the right person to say what to watch for in your own dog.

What do Shih Tzus usually die of?

No single cause dominates. In the VetCompass study, 89 Shih Tzus died during the study year. The most common recorded causes were gut disease, heart disease and poor quality of life, each in 7.9% of those deaths, and 18% had no cause recorded at all.

That is a small number of deaths, so treat the list as rough. Of the 85 deaths where the method was recorded, 94.1% involved euthanasia, which is typical of these vet-record studies.

Do male or female Shih Tzus live longer?

About the same. In UK vet records, females that died had a median age of 12.6 years and males 12.7, and the difference was not significant. In the Banfield data, lean neutered males and females both had a median of 14.5 years.

You may read that females live a year and a half longer. We found no study that shows it for Shih Tzus.

When does a Shih Tzu become a senior?

At about 9 to 10. The American Animal Hospital Association defines the senior stage as the last 25% of a dog's estimated lifespan. Using the UK medians of 12.7 and 12.8 years, 75% is about 9.5 to 9.6.

VCA Animal Hospitals uses weight instead, and puts dogs of 20 lb or less at about 9 to 11. The AKC gives Shih Tzus 9 to 16 lb, and the mean adult weight in the UK vet records was 7.9 kg (about 17 lb), so both methods point the same way. Some pages say 7 or 8; we found no source for that. Our senior dog age chart by breed explains both methods, and dog years to human years covers the age conversion.

How to help a Shih Tzu live longer

  1. Keep your dog lean. In the Banfield study, overweight Shih Tzus had medians about 0.6 to 0.7 years shorter than normal-weight ones, in both sexes. Ask your vet whether yours is carrying extra.
  2. Look after the teeth. Gum disease was the single most common problem in UK vet records, and dental problems as a group affected 13.3% of Shih Tzus in one year. Ask your vet about brushing and checks.
  3. Watch the eyes. Eye problems were common in the UK records, and corneal ulcers reached 3.5%. If an eye looks wrong to you, ask your vet.
  4. From around 9 or 10, book vet checks every six months instead of once a year, as VCA recommends for senior dogs.

Questions people ask

What is the average lifespan of a Shih Tzu?

About 12 to 13 years. A 2024 Dogs Trust study of UK dogs found a median of 12.8 years for 12,007 Shih Tzus, against 12.5 for all dogs. A 2024 VetCompass study of UK vet records gave a median age at death of 12.7 years. The American Kennel Club lists 10 to 18 years.

Do female Shih Tzus live longer than males?

Not in the one study with figures by sex. In UK vet records, females that died had a median age of 12.6 years and males 12.7, and the authors found no significant difference. In North American records of lean, neutered dogs, both sexes had a median of 14.5 years.

Can a Shih Tzu live 20 years?

It is very rare. The oldest Shih Tzu in the 2024 VetCompass study died at 19.9 years, and a quarter of the dogs lived past 14.3. A 23-year-old Shih Tzu is often quoted online, but we could not find a record to confirm it.

What do Shih Tzus usually die of?

No single cause stands out. In the 2024 VetCompass study, the most common recorded causes were gut disease, heart disease and poor quality of life, each in 7.9% of deaths. The study had only 89 deaths, so the figures are rough.

At what age is a Shih Tzu a senior?

At about 9 to 10. The American Animal Hospital Association puts the senior stage in the last 25% of a dog's expected lifespan, and 75% of 12.7 to 12.8 years is about 9.5 to 9.6. VCA's weight guide puts dogs of 20 lb or less at about 9 to 11. Ask your vet where your own dog sits.
