No, dogs shouldn't eat pumpkin pie. The pumpkin isn't the problem; the rest of the slice is. The filling carries sugar, fat and dairy that can upset a dog's stomach, the spice mix includes nutmeg, and a sugar-free pie may contain xylitol, which is toxic to dogs. Plain cooked pumpkin or canned 100% pumpkin is the version your dog can share.

If your dog has already eaten some, skip to the section on what to do now. The rest of this page goes through the pie part by part, so you know which slice is a stomach ache and which one is a phone call.

Why is pumpkin pie bad for dogs?

The AKC's answer is short: dogs shouldn't eat pumpkin pie, because the filling holds "plenty of sugars, fats, and dairy" on top of the spices, and those can upset a dog's stomach. Its Thanksgiving guide puts it the same way: pumpkin pie should remain a people-only dessert.

Dairy is its own small problem. The ASPCA explains that pets don't have much lactase, the enzyme that breaks down milk sugar, so milk and dairy products can cause diarrhea or other digestive upset. Evaporated milk, cream and whipped topping all count.

Then there is what else ends up in or on the pie. A pecan crumble, a chocolate drizzle, a bourbon glaze or a sugar-free filling each changes the answer, and we cover them in the table below.

Is nutmeg in pumpkin pie toxic to dogs?

Nutmeg contains a toxin called myristicin. That part is not in doubt. How worried to be about one slice is where the sources differ in tone.

Pet Poison Helpline says the small amount of nutmeg used in recipes is very unlikely to cause serious toxicity, though mild stomach upset could occur. A pet would need to eat a very large amount, which is very unlikely from food with nutmeg in it. In a very large amount, myristicin can cause hallucinations, disorientation, a fast heart rate, high blood pressure, dry mouth, abdominal pain and possibly seizures, and the signs can last up to 48 hours.

The AKC is stricter: it says dogs shouldn't have nutmeg at all, and lists the signs to watch for if your dog ate from the nutmeg shaker or "helped themselves to a serving of pumpkin pie": disorientation, dry mouth, vomiting, tremors and lack of coordination.

Both are right about different things. A slice is mostly a sugar-and-fat problem. The spice jar on the counter, or a bottle of nutmeg or pumpkin spice essential oil, is the real nutmeg risk: Pet Poison Helpline says the danger is highest when a pet gets into essential oils or the spices directly.

Sugar-free pumpkin pie and xylitol

This is the version to take most seriously. Xylitol is a sugar substitute found in sugar-free baked goods, gum, candy and some puddings, sauces and syrups. Pet Poison Helpline says that if enough is eaten it can cause life-threatening low blood sugar, even within 10 to 15 minutes, and acute liver failure. The ASPCA's Thanksgiving page names desserts with artificial sweeteners like xylitol as a risk of low blood sugar, liver damage or death.

The AKC also warns that canned pumpkin pie mix may contain xylitol. If a pie came from a shop or a "light" recipe, read the label before you decide how worried to be.

My dog ate pumpkin pie: what do I do now?

With an ordinary pie, the sources above point to an upset stomach more than poisoning. You still want a professional to hear the details, because the label decides the answer.

  1. Take what is left away and check that your dog is breathing and acting normally.
  2. Find the box, label or recipe. Look for xylitol, chocolate, raisins, nuts and alcohol.
  3. Work out roughly how much is gone. The ASPCA asks owners to note the amount eaten.
  4. Call your vet. Out of hours, call ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435 (the ASPCA says a consultation fee may apply) or Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661 (its site lists a $99 per-incident fee that includes follow-up calls).
  5. Don't make your dog vomit unless your vet or the poison line tells you to. Pet Poison Helpline says this plainly, and the AKC says this is no time for home remedies.
  6. If the pie had xylitol, don't wait for signs. The AKC's advice for toxic holiday foods is to call promptly instead of waiting for symptoms.

Can dogs eat plain pumpkin instead?

Yes. The AKC says dogs can eat pure pumpkin, and most of them enjoy it: it is low in fat and high in fiber. Use plain cooked pumpkin or canned pumpkin whose ingredients list says 100% pumpkin and nothing else. Pumpkin pie filling and canned pie mix can look a lot like plain pumpkin, so read the can.

More is not better. Pet Poison Helpline notes that pumpkin itself is not toxic but can cause stomach upset, especially diarrhea, because of its fiber. If your dog is older, on a special diet or on medication, ask your vet how much pumpkin suits them before it goes into the bowl.

Pumpkin pie and an older dog on the holiday table

The pie is rarely eaten from a plate you hand over. It is the slice on the coffee table, the plate a guest puts on the floor, the crust in the trash. A few habits cover most of it.

  • Tell guests before dessert that the dog doesn't get pie, and why.
  • Keep plates above nose height, and clear the dessert table before people settle on the sofa.
  • Empty the kitchen trash during the day. The ASPCA warns that holiday garbage fills with bones, chocolate, onions and other hazards.
  • Give your dog a quiet room with a blanket if the house gets busy, as the ASPCA suggests for guests.
  • Give your dog their own treat at dessert time: a spoon of plain pumpkin, if your vet has said yes.

The ASPCA recommends keeping your pet's diet as close to normal as you can, and suggests giving dogs a small feast of their own: pieces of boneless, fully cooked turkey, sweet potato and green beans mixed into their usual dinner. A holiday bowl gets the full routine, the stare, the feet, the noise when you are slow.

Questions people ask

Can dogs eat pumpkin pie?

No. The AKC says pumpkin pie should stay a people-only dessert. The filling holds sugar, fat and dairy that can upset a dog's stomach, the spice mix includes nutmeg, and sugar-free versions may contain xylitol, which is toxic to dogs.

My dog ate a slice of pumpkin pie. Should I panic?

No, but act. Check the recipe or box for xylitol, chocolate, raisins or nuts, note how much was eaten, and call your vet or a pet poison line. Pet Poison Helpline says the nutmeg in recipes is very unlikely to cause serious toxicity, though mild stomach upset can happen.

How much nutmeg is toxic to dogs?

Neither the AKC nor Pet Poison Helpline gives an amount for owners. Pet Poison Helpline says a pet would need a very large amount, which is very unlikely from food, and that the risk is highest from the spice itself or essential oils. If your dog got into the jar, call your vet now.

Can dogs eat pumpkin pie filling or canned pie mix?

No. The AKC says dogs shouldn't eat canned pumpkin pie mix or pie filling, because the sugars, fats and dairy are already mixed in. Buy canned pumpkin whose ingredients list says 100% pumpkin and nothing else.

Can dogs eat pie crust?

The AKC says a taste of crust won't harm your dog, but too much butter or shortening can upset digestion or trigger pancreatitis. Pecan or chocolate crusts and toppings bring their own risks.
