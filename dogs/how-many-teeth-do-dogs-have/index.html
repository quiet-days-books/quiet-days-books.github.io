Adult dogs have 42 teeth: 12 incisors, 4 canines, 16 premolars and 10 molars. Puppies have 28 baby teeth, which start coming in at 3 to 5 weeks. The adult teeth begin to replace them at around 4 to 5 months, and by 7 months the full adult set is usually in.

How many teeth does an adult dog have?

Forty-two. The Merck Veterinary Manual splits them like this:

The odd split between the jaws comes from the molars. The lower jaw has one extra molar on each side, so a dog has 20 teeth on top and 22 below. A count of fewer than 42 in an adult dog can mean a tooth has been lost, broken or removed, or never came in.

How many teeth do puppies have?

Puppies have 28 baby teeth, which vets call deciduous teeth. Merck says they begin to come in at 3 to 5 weeks of age, and a 2023 study notes that most breeds have all 28 by about twelve weeks.

The jump from 28 to 42 happens between about 4 and 7 months. The adult teeth form under the baby teeth and press on their roots, which slowly dissolve until the baby tooth falls out and the adult tooth takes its place.

When do puppies lose their baby teeth?

Usually between about 4 and 7 months. Merck puts the start of the adult teeth at around 4 to 5 months and says all of them are in by 7 months.

The most detailed timetable we found comes from a 2023 study of 61 Yorkshire Terrier puppies, checked every four weeks. For a puppy of about 4.5 kg, the middle of the group, the estimated ages were:

Look at the canines. In these estimates the adult canine started to come in about two weeks before the baby canine fell out, so for a while the puppy had two fangs side by side. Two teeth in one spot is what vets call a retained tooth, covered below. In the smallest puppies, 3 kg or under, everything happened later and the usual order was disrupted.

Do small dogs have fewer teeth?

No. A Chihuahua and a Great Dane both grow 42 adult teeth. The small dog fits them into a much smaller jaw, and that has consequences.

VCA Animal Hospitals says small breeds and short-nosed (brachycephalic) dogs are more prone to periodontal disease because of the small size of the jaw and crowding of the teeth. Merck adds that judging a small or toy dog's age from its teeth is more difficult than for a large dog.

The UK VetCompass breed studies show how common tooth problems are in small dogs. In Yorkshire Terriers seen by vets in 2016, periodontal disease was the most common problem recorded in a year, in 21.1% of dogs, and persistent baby teeth came fifth, at 3.57%. Our page on how long Yorkies live has more from that study, and how long Chihuahuas live covers the other breed that comes up most often here.

What is a retained baby tooth?

A baby tooth that is still in the mouth when the adult tooth that replaces it is already there. Vets call it a persistent deciduous tooth.

It is especially common in small and toy breeds. In the 2023 Yorkshire Terrier study, 69% of the puppies had at least one. Canines were kept most often: 89.1% of puppies weighing 3 kg or less kept a baby canine, against 12.0% of puppies over 5 kg. The same paper cites a UK study of 11,647 Chihuahuas where persistent baby teeth were recorded in 5.7%, and in 11.3% of those under two.

Why it matters, according to the study authors:

  • the crowded spot traps plaque, which can lead to gum disease
  • the adult tooth can be pushed off its path, so the bite does not line up
  • a lower canine that comes in at the wrong angle can press on the roof of the mouth

Treatment is usually removal of the baby tooth by a vet. The authors recommend regular vet checks for Yorkshire Terriers between two and seven months. If you can see two teeth in one spot for more than a short while, ask your vet to look.

What do the numbers on a vet's dental chart mean?

If your dog has a dental cleaning, the report may list teeth by three-digit numbers such as 104 or 409. This is the modified Triadan system, which Merck describes.

The first digit is the quarter of the mouth. 100 is the upper right, 200 the upper left, 300 the lower left and 400 the lower right. The last two digits count teeth from the middle of the mouth outward, so 101 is the front incisor on the upper right and 104 is the upper right canine. Baby teeth use 500 to 800 for the same four quarters. A missing tooth keeps its number; the count simply skips it.

How common is dental disease in dogs?

Very common. VCA Animal Hospitals says over 80% of dogs older than three have active dental disease, and that more than two-thirds of dogs over three have some degree of periodontal disease, the infection and inflammation of the tissues around the tooth. Tooth decay, the main problem in people, is rare in dogs: less than 10% of dental problems.

Few dogs show obvious signs. When they do, VCA lists:

  • bad breath
  • pawing at the mouth or shaking the head
  • chewing with obvious discomfort or dropping food
  • drooling a lot, sometimes with blood in the saliva

VCA says the best way to prevent tartar is daily brushing with a toothpaste made for dogs and designed to be swallowed. Once tartar has formed, it needs scaling and polishing by a vet under general anaesthesia. Your vet can tell you how your dog's teeth look and what care they need.

Teeth in an older dog

Merck notes that for most large adult dogs, vets can estimate age from the pattern of wear on the teeth. Questions people ask

How many teeth do dogs have?

Adult dogs have 42 permanent teeth: 12 incisors, 4 canines, 16 premolars and 10 molars. There are 20 in the upper jaw and 22 in the lower. Puppies have 28 baby teeth, which are replaced by the adult set between about 4 and 7 months of age.

Do small dogs have fewer teeth than big dogs?

No. A Chihuahua and a Great Dane both have 42 adult teeth. Small dogs fit them into a much smaller jaw, which is one reason VCA says small breeds are more prone to periodontal disease.

When do puppies lose their baby teeth?

Usually between about 4 and 7 months. Merck says adult teeth start appearing at around 4 to 5 months and are all in by 7 months. In a 2023 study of Yorkshire Terriers, the baby incisors went first, at about 22 weeks, and the baby canines last, at about 26 weeks.

Is it normal for a puppy to have two teeth in one spot?

For a short time it can happen while the adult tooth pushes the baby one out. If the baby tooth stays once the adult tooth is coming through, it is called a persistent (or retained) deciduous tooth, and vets often remove it. Ask your vet to look if it lasts.

Do dogs have wisdom teeth?

No, according to PetMD. In a 2023 study of Yorkshire Terriers, the molars were the last adult teeth to come in, at about 26 weeks, so the full set is in place well before the first birthday.
