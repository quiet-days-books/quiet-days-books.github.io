In short

  • A tail held straight up, often with a curved tip, is a friendly greeting to a cat, another animal or a person.
  • A tail tucked under or wrapped tight to the body usually means the cat is worried or afraid.
  • A puffed tail with an arched, sideways body means stay away; a tail held out and swept slowly side to side can mean frustration.
  • Read the tail with the ears, the body and the situation, never on its own.
  • A tail that suddenly hangs limp can also be a sign the cat is hurt or unwell; ask your vet.

A cat's tail is one of its clearest signals. Held straight up, it says hello. Tucked under or wrapped tight, it says the cat is worried. Puffed up over an arched, sideways body, it says stay away. Held out and swept slowly from side to side, it can mean frustration.

The tail is only one part of the message, though. Below is the chart, then what research adds to it, then how to read your own cat's version.

Cats Protection makes the point that context is the better guide to whether a cat is happy and comfortable. A tail that looks the same can mean different things in a quiet living room and at the vet's.

What does it mean when a cat's tail is straight up?

It is a greeting. International Cat Care describes the tail-up position, held vertically at a right angle to the ground, as a sign of friendly intent when a cat approaches another cat, another animal or a person. The RSPCA adds the detail many owners recognise: a happy cat standing with its tail upright and the tip curved.

There is research behind this. In 2009, Simona Cafazzo and Eugenia Natoli watched a group of free-living cats in Rome and recorded who raised a tail to whom. Lower-ranking cats showed the tail-up signal more often, and higher-ranking cats received it more often. The authors read it as a cat saying, in effect, that it is friendly and knows where it stands. The study also confirmed that tail-up goes together with other friendly behaviour.

So when your cat trots towards you with its tail up, it is using the same greeting it would offer a cat it gets on with. Often it ends with a rub against your legs, which International Cat Care describes as the same as a greeting between two cats.

Why does my cat wag its tail?

Cats wag for other reasons than dogs do. International Cat Care says a tail held out and moving slowly side to side across the body may signal frustration. If you are stroking your cat when the tail starts to sweep, that is a good moment to stop and let it decide what happens next.

A crouching cat thumping its tail on the floor is in a classic defensive posture, according to the same source. Give it room.

Why does my cat puff up its tail?

To look bigger. Raising the hairs, called piloerection, makes the cat's outline larger. International Cat Care notes that some cats puff up their tail when suddenly frightened and then go to investigate whatever startled them.

With an arched back and a sideways stance, a bushy tail is a different message. That is a hostile "stay away" signal meant to increase distance. The kindest response is to step back, keep your hands to yourself and let the cat leave or settle.

Tucked, wrapped and low: the worried tail

A tail tucked between the back legs is often a sign that a cat is anxious or fearful, International Cat Care says. The RSPCA describes a worried cat crouched with its tail tucked tightly into its body, and an angry or very unhappy cat lying with tail and legs held tight in.

Cats Protection lists other signs that often come with it: ears to the side, back or flat, wide pupils, hiding or getting up high. If you see this, look for what changed in the room. A new noise, a visitor or another animal is a common cause. Give the cat a quiet way out.

A tail that suddenly hangs limp or drags, and stays that way, is a different matter. It can also be a sign the cat is hurt or unwell, and the Cat Fanciers' Association advises taking any cat with an unusual tail to the vet promptly. Ask your vet.

What does it mean when a cat wraps its tail around you?

The AAFP and ISFM guidelines list tail wrapping among the behaviours that cats in the same social group show each other, together with rubbing, grooming each other, sleeping close and playing together. A cat that curls its tail around your leg or your arm is treating you in a similar way.

Read the tail with the ears and the body

The tail is one signal among several. Cats Protection describes a happy cat with ears forward and relaxed, a relaxed posture and slow blinks, and a stressed cat with flat ears. A tail held up with forward ears is a confident greeting. The same tail with flattened ears and puffed fur is not.

Sometimes the tail says nothing at all. In a 2013 study by Atsuko Saito and Kazutaka Shinozuka, 20 cats heard voices calling their names, including their owner's. The cats answered with their ears and head, turning towards the sound, rather than with their tail or a meow. That held for the owner's voice too. A cat that stays put with a still tail but swivels an ear has heard you.

How to learn your own cat's tail

Charts give the common readings. Your cat has its own habits, and the only way to learn them is to watch in context. Our book The House Your Cat Knows has two activities that fit:

  • The Greeting They Offer. The next time you come home, notice what your cat actually offers. A tail, a sound, a glance, a walk past your legs or no ceremony at all. Write it down without upgrading it, and don't call the cat to make it happen.
  • The Look You Know. Describe one familiar look, posture or pause, with what was going on at the time. The book's rule for this page fits tails too: don't turn the signal into a universal rule or a medical conclusion.

A few notes over a week usually show a pattern: the tail at the door, the tail at the food bowl, the tail when a visitor arrives. Questions people ask

What does it mean when a cat's tail is straight up?

A tail held vertically, often with the tip curved over, is a friendly signal. International Cat Care describes it as friendly intent when a cat approaches another cat, animal or person, and the RSPCA lists it as a sign of a happy cat.

What does it mean when a cat wags its tail?

Cats wag for other reasons than dogs do. International Cat Care says a tail held out and moving slowly side to side across the body may signal frustration. Look at the ears and body too, and give the cat space if it seems tense.

Why does my cat puff up its tail?

Raising the fur makes the cat look bigger. Some cats puff their tail when suddenly startled. With an arched back and a sideways stance, a bushy tail is a clear 'stay away' signal.

What does it mean when a cat wraps its tail around you?

Tail wrapping is one of the friendly behaviours that cats in the same social group show each other, alongside rubbing and grooming, according to the AAFP and ISFM. When a cat does it to you, it is treating you in a similar way.

My cat doesn't move its tail when I call. Is it ignoring me?

Not necessarily. In a 2013 study, cats responded to their owner's voice by moving their ears and head, not their tail. A still tail with turned ears means the cat heard you.
