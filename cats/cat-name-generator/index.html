How the cat name generator works

The draw is random and runs in your browser. Nothing you choose is saved or sent anywhere. With JavaScript switched off the form does not appear, and the table gives you every name anyway.

It is not a popularity ranking. We have no data on which cat names are common this year, so we do not label any name as popular.

We left out house plants and cut flowers on purpose. Lily is a pretty name, and it is also a plant the ASPCA lists as toxic to cats. The name itself harms nobody, but we would rather not send anyone looking at lilies. Our page on Christmas cat names explains which seasonal plant names are fine to say and better kept out of the house.

How to choose between the names you get

A name has to survive being said many times a day, by everyone in the house. A few ways to test a short list:

  • Say each name the way you would call it from another room. Some look good written down and feel silly out loud.
  • Use one for a full day before you decide. By evening you will know.
  • Ask everyone who lives with the cat. A name only one person says turns into a nickname.
  • Look at the cat while you say it. A name taken from something you can see is easier to keep.
  • If your cat already has a name, treat the draw as a second name for the season or for the kitchen.

If none of the names fits, change the starting point. A grey cat who sprints down the hall at night may be more of a Rocket than a Pewter.

Do cats know their names?

They can tell them apart from other words. In 2019 Atsuko Saito and colleagues published a study in Scientific Reports in which cats heard a series of ordinary nouns, or the names of the cats they lived with, and then their own name. Cats from ordinary households reacted again when their own name came, and they did so even when a stranger said it.

The study measured noticing, not obeying. A cat who flicks an ear at their name and stays on the chair has still heard you. For more on what cats keep in mind, see our page on how good a cat's memory is, and our fun facts about cats has more findings like this one.

The names you end up using

The official name is rarely the whole story. Our book The House Your Cat Knows has a page for this, "The Names Used in This House". It starts from the observation that a cat may have one official name and twelve household names with no official use, and asks you to write down the names, nicknames and small phrases you really use, who says them, and which one feels most like your home.

A new name is a good moment to start that list, before the nicknames pile up. For other small things to notice once the name is settled, see our list of things to do with your cat.

Questions people ask

How does the cat name generator pick names?

You choose a starting point, such as a black coat, the way your cat moves or the sounds they make, and the page draws a few names at random from that part of our list. Press again for a new draw. The draw happens in your browser and nothing is saved or sent.

Are these the most popular cat names?

No. It is our own list of ordinary words that suit a cat, and we have no data on how often any of them is used. If you want a name few other cats have, that may be a point in its favor.

Do cats recognize their own name?

A 2019 study by Atsuko Saito and colleagues in Scientific Reports found that cats from ordinary households told their own name apart from other nouns, even when a stranger said it. The study measured whether cats noticed the name, not whether they come when called.

Can a cat have more than one name?

In most homes it already does: one official name and a handful of nicknames nobody planned. Use the generator for a second name if your cat is already named, and write down the ones that stick.
