In short

  • Cats remember well what matters to them: food, places, routes and the people they live with.
  • Memory for something that vanishes from sight is short: in one study, accuracy dropped quickly between 0 and 30 seconds.
  • From a single meal, cats kept 'what' and 'where' for at least 15 minutes in a Kyoto University study.
  • Cats can tell their owner's voice from strangers' and seem to picture where the owner is from the voice alone.
  • No study has measured how many years a cat remembers a person; claims of exact spans are guesses.

A cat's memory is good for what matters to it. Cats remember what they ate and where after a single meal, know their owner's voice from strangers' and keep track of where their people are. Their memory for something that disappears from view is short, fading within seconds.

Below is what each kind of memory looks like in studies, what nobody has measured yet, and what that means for life at home.

The pattern is clear enough. A cat's memory is built around food, places and its people, not around keeping track of objects for their own sake.

What is a cat's memory span for things out of sight?

Short. In a 2006 study, Fiset and Doré trained 24 cats to find a desirable object hidden behind one of four boxes, then made them wait 0, 10, 30 or 60 seconds before searching. The cats' accuracy dropped quickly between 0 and 30 seconds, though it stayed better than chance at 60. Visual cues on the boxes did not help them remember.

This may be why a toy that rolls under the sofa can be searched for eagerly and then, soon after, apparently forgotten. Our article on why your cat brings you toys covers what happens with the toys it does keep track of.

Do cats remember what happened, not just what they learned?

There is evidence they do. In a study at Kyoto University published in 2017, cats were led to four food containers and allowed to eat from two of them. After a 15-minute break out of the room, they were brought back without warning.

In the second experiment, two containers held food, one held an object that could not be eaten, and one was empty, and each cat ate from only one. When they came back, the cats went first to the container that still held food they hadn't eaten more often than chance, and spent longer there. The researchers concluded that the cats had kept both "what" and "where" from a single event they had no reason to memorise. Scientists call this kind of memory episodic-like.

Do cats remember their owners?

They know their owners' voices. In a 2013 study, Atsuko Saito and Kazutaka Shinozuka played 20 cats recordings of three strangers calling their names, then their owner. Fifteen of the 20 reacted less to the third stranger than to the first. Those 15 cats then reacted clearly again when they heard their owner's voice. The cats answered with their ears and heads rather than their tails or a meow, which is why a cat can recognise you and still not get up.

A later study went further. In 2021, Takagi and colleagues played a cat its owner's voice from a speaker outside the room several times, then once from a speaker on the far side of the room. The cats looked most surprised when the same owner suddenly seemed to be somewhere else. That reaction did not appear with cat sounds or other noises. The authors say cats appear to keep a mental picture of where their out-of-sight owner is, linked to the owner's voice.

This is part of why a cat may be waiting at the door before you open it, and why it may follow you around the house. See our articles on the cat daily routine and on why your cat follows you.

Cat long-term memory: how long does it last?

Here the honest answer is that nobody knows the number. Websites give figures such as ten years or a whole lifetime, but we could not find a study that measured how many years a cat remembers a person, a place or an event.

What studies do show is that cats learn and keep the things they use daily: their routes through the house, where food appears, and the voices of the people they live with.

Do cats remember places and routes?

A cat that takes the same path from the bedroom to the kitchen each morning is relying on what it has learned about the house. Our book The House Your Cat Knows opens with an activity built on exactly this, A Map of Their Routes: watch one normal route without directing it, then sketch it later, marking rooms, thresholds, pauses and the places your cat avoids.

Another, The Time They Arrive, asks you to notice when your cat tends to turn up near you, in the kitchen or by the bed, and what changes in the room when it does. Neither activity tests your cat. Both show you what it has already learned about your home and your day.

Does a cat's memory change with age?

The AAFP and ISFM guidelines note that a cat's preferences for contact with people may change as it ages and experiences sensory decline and reduced mobility. A cat that once loved being picked up may come to prefer being stroked where it rests.

The same guidelines add that changes in behaviour may also be linked to an underlying medical problem. If an older cat seems lost in rooms it knew well or its routine changes suddenly, it can be a sign the cat is unwell. Ask your vet.

Keeping the memories on your side

Your cat remembers the house in its own way. Your side of that is yours to keep: the route, the window, the sound of arrival, the names you use. Questions people ask

Do cats have a good memory?

For the things that matter to them, yes. Studies show cats remember what they ate and where after a single experience, recognise their owner's voice and keep track of where their owner is. Their memory for an object that disappears from view is short.

How long is a cat's memory?

It depends on the kind of memory. In one study, cats' accuracy at finding a hidden object dropped quickly between 0 and 30 seconds, though it stayed above chance at 60 seconds. In another, cats used what they learned from one meal 15 minutes later. Long-term spans in years have not been measured.

Do cats have long-term memory?

Yes, in the sense that they learn and keep routines, routes, places and people. Recognising an owner's voice relies on it. What no study has shown is a reliable number of years.

Do cats remember people?

They remember the people they live with. In a 2013 study, cats that had stopped reacting to strangers' voices reacted again to their owner's. How long a cat remembers someone it has not seen for years has not been tested.

Do older cats lose their memory?

The AAFP and ISFM note that a cat's preferences can change as it ages, and that changes in behaviour can also be linked to a medical problem. If an older cat seems lost in places it knew well, ask your vet.
