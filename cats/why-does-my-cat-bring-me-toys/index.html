In short

  • A toy dropped at your feet is usually the end of a pretend hunt, carried to the safest place a cat knows: home, near you.
  • It is often an invitation too. Many cats fetch without being taught, and they mostly decide when the game starts and stops.
  • Real prey brought home is not a gift either, says Cats Protection; the home is where the cat feels safe.
  • Toys at night or on the bed fit a cat's natural busy times around dusk and dawn.
  • Answer with a few minutes of play that ends in a catch, then a small treat.

Your cat brings you toys because carrying a "catch" back to a safe place is part of how cats hunt, and you are part of that safe place. Often it is also an invitation: many cats start fetching without being taught, and they mostly decide when the game begins and when it ends.

So a toy at your feet is rarely random. Here is what is going on, and what to do about it.

Why does my cat bring me toys? The hunting part

The AAFP and ISFM guidelines describe a cat's predatory sequence as locating, stalking, chasing, pouncing, killing, preparing and eating prey. They add that this behaviour happens even in well-fed cats. A toy mouse can stand in for the real thing at every step.

Carrying the catch somewhere safe belongs to the same sequence. Writing about real prey, Cats Protection says cats bring it home because home is where they feel safe and secure, and where other animals can't steal it. An indoor cat with a toy is doing the same thing on a smaller scale. The safest spot in its territory may well be the sofa next to you.

Why does my cat bring me toys and meow? An invitation to play

Some cats deliver a toy and wait. Then they do it again. That is fetch, and studies of it say a lot about who is running the game.

In a 2023 survey published in Scientific Reports, Forman and colleagues asked 924 owners about 1,154 cats that fetch. Nearly all the owners, 94.4%, said the fetching began without any training. The cats started and ended fetching sessions more often than their owners did. The authors concluded that cats who fetch largely decide when they play and shape what their owners do.

A larger 2024 study looked at owner questionnaires for 8,224 cats. Fetching was reported in 40.9% of them. Cats that fetched tended to be more playful and active. Breeds from the Far East, such as Siamese, Burmese and Tonkinese, fetched more often, while female cats, older cats and cats with health problems were less likely to.

So if your cat drops a toy and looks at you, the most likely meaning is the simplest one: your turn.

Why does my cat bring me things that aren't toys?

Owners in the 2023 survey reported a wide range of fetched objects, not only toys bought for the purpose. To a cat, something light enough to carry can play the part of prey.

Some of these are worth putting away. The AAFP and ISFM advise that toys with string or parts a cat could swallow are put away after play, and that small parts and bells are removed before unsupervised play. Our book says the same on its chosen-object page: no strings, ribbons or small loose parts left unattended.

Cat brings toys to bed at night

Cats are crepuscular, most active at dusk and dawn, the PDSA says, which comes from their hunting instinct. A cat with energy to spend at those hours may well bring its catch to where you are, and at night that is the bed.

A play session in the evening often helps. The AAFP and ISFM suggest moving a wand toy like a bird or a mouse, letting the cat catch it at the end, and then giving a treat, which completes the sequence of hunt, catch and eat. Our article on the cat daily routine covers how those dawn and dusk peaks shape the rest of a cat's day.

Do cats bring mice as a gift?

Not in the way we mean it. Cats Protection says plainly that cats are not bringing you a gift with prey; they bring it home because home is safe. It also says you shouldn't punish a cat for hunting or bringing prey home, since shouting won't change a natural instinct and the stress can cause other problems.

Play makes a measurable difference here. In a University of Exeter trial published in 2021, households that added 5 to 10 minutes of daily play with toys saw a 25% drop in the number of animals their cats caught and brought home. Cats Protection gives similar advice: redirect the need to hunt onto suitable toys, with short play sessions built into the daily routine.

What to do when your cat brings you a toy

  1. Take it as an invitation. A few minutes is enough.
  2. Throw or drag it away from the cat, along the floor, the way prey would run.
  3. Let the cat catch it at the end, and finish with a small treat.
  4. Stop while the game still feels easy, and let the cat walk away.
  5. Rotate toys. The guidelines suggest using toys in rotation so the cat doesn't get bored of them.
  6. Keep hands and feet out of the game, which the guidelines advise to avoid injury.

If the toy arrives while you are busy, you don't have to answer at once. Play later works too. For more ideas, see our guide to things to do with your cat.

Keep a record of the game your cat already plays

Every cat that brings toys has its own version: one particular toy, one time of day, one spot where it drops it. Our book The House Your Cat Knows has two activities for this. The Game They Already Know asks you to record a play pattern your cat already knows, keep it short and stop while the game still feels easy. Their Chosen Object asks you to photograph one object your cat picks again and again, and write what it does with it and how long it has been part of the house.

A toy that has been carried to you again and again is worth keeping a picture of. Our cat keepsake ideas guide has ways to keep things like that. Questions people ask

Why does my cat bring me toys?

Carrying a 'catch' back to a safe place is part of a cat's hunting sequence, and you and your home are that safe place. Many cats also use it to start a game: in a 2023 survey, cats began and ended fetching sessions more often than their owners did.

Why does my cat bring me toys at night?

Cats are crepuscular, most active at dusk and dawn, according to the PDSA. A cat with energy to spend at those times may carry a toy to wherever you are, including the bed. A short play session before you go to sleep can help.

Why does my cat bring me things that aren't toys?

Owners in the 2023 fetching survey described a wide range of objects being fetched. To a cat, anything small enough to carry can stand in for prey. Put away string, ribbon and small parts, which the AAFP and ISFM advise keeping out of reach.

Do cats bring mice as a gift?

Cats Protection says no: cats bring prey home because home is where they feel safe and secure, and they may want to eat it undisturbed. Don't punish a cat for it; shouting won't stop a natural instinct.

How do I stop my cat bringing me dead animals?

Play helps. In a University of Exeter trial, households that added 5 to 10 minutes of daily play with toys saw 25% fewer animals brought home. Cats Protection also suggests short, regular play sessions with suitable toys.
