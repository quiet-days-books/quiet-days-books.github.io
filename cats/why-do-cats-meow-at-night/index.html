In short

  • Cats are most active around dusk and dawn, so the hours either side of your sleep are their busy time.
  • Adult cats rarely meow at each other. A meow at 4 a.m. is almost always meant for you.
  • Hunger, boredom and a learned habit are the common reasons. A yowl is a hostile sound, aimed at another cat or animal.
  • Play before bed, smaller meals spread through the day and not rewarding the night meow help most cats.
  • If night meowing is new, or the sound itself has changed, ask your vet.

Cats meow at night because their natural active hours peak around dusk and dawn, which overlap with the edges of our sleep, and because meowing is how adult cats ask people for things. A night meow usually means the cat wants food or attention, has energy to spend, or has learned that meowing at that hour gets a response.

Why are cats awake at night?

Cats are not strictly night animals. Cats Protection explains that they are naturally most active at dawn and dusk, young cats especially, like their wild relative the African wildcat, which hunts small prey at those times. So the hours just before you go to bed and just before you wake are when a cat is most likely to be up and looking for something to do.

A 2022 study measured this. Researchers in France tracked cats for 21 days in July, some living indoors at a research cattery and some in a large outdoor enclosure at an animal shelter, using sensors that recorded how far each cat moved and when it ate. Both groups showed peaks of activity and feeding around twilight. Both also moved and ate more when people were present, and the outdoor cats were more active at night than the indoor ones.

Your cat's day is partly built around yours, which is one reason it may come looking for you at hours you would rather spend asleep. Our article on why cats sleep so much explains where all the daytime naps fit.

Who is the meow for?

You, almost always. International Cat Care says that in most circumstances meows are directed at people rather than other cats, and that adult cats rarely meow at each other. Cats Protection calls meowing a learned behaviour cats use with humans because it usually gets a positive response.

This is why each cat ends up with its own vocabulary. Cats Protection describes a cat developing its own range of meows that its owner learns to recognise, for example a short meow for attention and a longer one for food. If your cat has a particular 4 a.m. meow, it probably developed that one with you.

Most cats have more than one of these going at once, which is why a single fix rarely works on its own.

Why answering the meow can make it worse

If a meow at 4 a.m. gets you up to fill the bowl, the cat has learned something useful. International Cat Care notes that people reinforce meowing, which is how it develops into so many forms, and Cats Protection explains that a cat fed in answer to a meow learns that this meow means food.

So the first step is to stop paying for the meow at night with food. Food at 4 a.m. is the reward the cat is learning to ask for.

Don't swing the other way into scolding. Cats Protection advises against telling a cat off for waking you, because it could be trying to tell you it is unwell. A quiet non-response is the aim.

How to help your cat sleep at night

These come from Cats Protection's advice on cats at night:

  1. Play properly during the day and in the evening. A fishing rod toy lets your cat chase, pounce and tire itself out.
  2. Spread food out. Smaller, more regular meals through the day, or an automatic feeder set to open in the night or early morning, take hunger out of the 4 a.m. meow.
  3. Give an indoor cat things to do: toys, a cat tree, puzzle feeders, high perches, boxes and a scratching post.
  4. Keep a clean litter tray and fresh water, placed well apart.
  5. If you lose a lot of sleep and nothing helps, ask your vet for a referral to a qualified cat behaviourist.

Cats Protection also recommends keeping cats indoors at night for their safety, since more road injuries happen late at night and cats fight more over territory in the dark. A cat kept in will have more night energy to spend, so the play and the toys matter more.

When night meowing is a reason to see the vet

Cats Protection says some medical conditions can make cats wake in the night, cry a lot, or seem restless and disoriented. Its advice on meowing in general is that cats rarely change their behaviour for no reason, and that if the sounds themselves change, a vet visit is the best way to rule out a health problem. If your cat has started meowing at night when it never used to, or seems confused or unsettled, ask your vet.

Watching the rest of your cat helps you describe what you see. Cats Protection suggests looking at the ears: turned to the side or back rather than forward and relaxed, they can mean the cat is anxious. Our guide to cat body language covers the other signals.

Learn your cat's night pattern

Before changing anything, it helps to know when the meowing actually happens. Our book The House Your Cat Knows has two activities that work well as a two-week log:

  • "The Evening Route." Notice where your cat goes as the day turns into evening, and write the path, the place it chooses or the sound it makes when the house changes.
  • "The Time They Arrive." Write down one time your cat tends to turn up beside the bed, in the kitchen or at a door, and what usually changes in the room. The book's rule: do not call the cat in to prove the pattern.

A few nights of notes may show that the meow follows something specific, such as an empty bowl or a regular hour. Our cat daily routine guide shows a simple way to keep notes like these. Once you know the pattern, you know which row of the table to start with.

Questions people ask

Why does my cat meow at night for no reason?

There is usually a reason, even if it is not obvious. Cats are naturally most active around dawn and dusk, and International Cat Care says meows are mostly aimed at people. Common causes are hunger, spare energy or a habit the cat has learned because the meow got a response before.

Should I ignore my cat meowing at night?

Don't reward it with food, but don't punish it either. Cats Protection explains that cats learn meows that get a response, so getting up to feed or play teaches the night meow. It also advises against telling a cat off for waking you, because a cat can be trying to tell you it is unwell. If the meowing is new, ask your vet first.

Why do cats meow at 3 or 4 a.m.?

Early morning is close to the dawn peak in a cat's activity. Cats Protection says it is normal for cats to wake owners before dawn by meowing or pawing at their face, and many wake with the first light. If hunger is part of it, Cats Protection suggests smaller, more regular meals during the day.

How do I stop my cat meowing at night?

Play an active game in the evening, spread food through the day in smaller meals or use an automatic feeder for the early hours, and stop answering the meow with food. Cats Protection suggests a fishing rod toy for daytime play. If nothing changes, your vet can refer you to a qualified cat behaviourist.

Why does my cat yowl at night?

International Cat Care lists yowls among the hostile sounds cats make with the mouth held open, used in conflict with other cats or animals. Cats Protection adds that cats are more inclined to fight at night, and keeping your cat in after dark is its advice for safety. If the yowling is new, ask your vet.
