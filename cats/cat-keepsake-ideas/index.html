In short

  • The best cat keepsakes come from ordinary days: a whisker that fell out by itself, fur from the brush, a retired collar, the box or blanket they claimed.
  • Use only what your cat leaves behind. Never pull, cut or pluck fur or whiskers to make a keepsake.
  • Date everything and write where you found it. A whisker with a note is a record; a whisker alone is a mystery.
  • Sounds and words are keepsakes too: the arrival sound in the hall, the names your household actually uses.
  • A small box, a jar and a folder on your phone are enough. Nothing has to be bought.

Good cat keepsake ideas start with what your cat already leaves around the house: a whisker that fell out on its own, a tuft of fur from the brush, a collar retired when it wore out, the box they claimed and would not give back. Keep only what your cat drops naturally, write the date and the place beside it, and add a few seconds of sound.

That is the whole approach. The rest of this page is the detail: what to keep, how to store it so it lasts, and what to leave alone.

Cat whisker keepsake ideas

Finding a whisker on the floor is a small, odd moment, and plenty of people keep them. Our book The House Your Cat Knows has a page for exactly this, "The Natural Keepsake". It asks you to photograph a trace you find naturally, or write down where it was, and lets you keep it if that feels right. Its safety line is the one rule worth repeating: never pull, cut or extract fur or whiskers. Use only what is naturally found.

Whiskers are stiff and bend easily, so storage matters more than with fur:

  • A small glass vial with a cork, the kind sold for craft beads.
  • A matchbox or tiny wooden box lined with tissue.
  • Taped flat inside a folded card, with the date and the room where it turned up.

Write the note at the time. "Found on the arm of the green chair, March" is what makes a whisker yours instead of any cat's. If you later want to turn one into jewellery or frame it with a photo, a dated whisker in a vial keeps safely until you decide.

The book's fallback is kind too: if there is no trace, skip the page. Nothing needs to be manufactured.

Fur, and the things they have worn into shape

You probably have more of your cat's fur than you want. Keeping a small amount on purpose is a different thing. Next time you brush them, save a pinch of what comes out in the brush, or lift a tuft from the blanket where they sleep. Put it in an envelope or a small jar and date it.

The worn things count as well. The book's "The Natural Keepsake" page mentions the worn corner of an object already loved into shape. A scratched cardboard edge, a flattened cushion corner, a blanket with a hollow in the middle: photograph them before they get replaced, and note how long they have been in the house.

Their chosen object

Some objects become important without anyone asking: the delivery box that should have been recycled months ago, a blanket corner, a toy that looks finished but apparently is not. "Their Chosen Object" in our book asks you to photograph the one your cat picks again and again, and to write what they do with it and how long it has been part of the house.

Of everything on this page, it is the one that shows a choice your cat made. Keep the object if it is safe to keep. The book's safety note is practical: strings, ribbons and small loose parts should not be left lying about with a cat around, so a frayed string toy belongs in a closed box rather than on a shelf.

If your cat sometimes brings you a toy, our page on why your cat brings you toys looks at what that habit means, and the toy they carry to you is a good candidate for this page.

The collar, when it is retired

If your cat wears a collar, the old one makes a natural keepsake when it is replaced, along with the tag. Photograph it flat in daylight first, the tag close enough to read, then put it in the box. Cover the phone number and address in any photo you share.

The time to retire a collar is set by safety, not by the keepsake. Cats Protection says the safest collars for cats are quick-release or snap-opening ones, that you should be able to fit two fingers under a fitted collar, and that a damaged collar should be replaced straight away. PDSA adds that a quick-release buckle can get blocked with dirt and grease, so check it regularly, and that collars with elastic inserts can cause serious injuries. Both charities point to a microchip as the most reliable way to be reunited with a lost cat. So the worn collar goes in the keepsake box, and the cat gets a new one that opens as it should.

Keepsakes you can hear and see

Some of the best cat keepsakes are not objects at all.

"The Sound of Arrival" in our book asks for a few seconds of the sound your cat makes coming into a room, recorded from a respectful distance when it happens naturally: feet on wood, a soft landing, a trill from the hallway. Its companion page, "The Soundtrack of the Room", is thirty seconds of a room your cat uses while nothing special is happening. For the purr itself, our guide on how to record cat purring covers phone placement and quiet rooms.

For pictures, "A Photo From Their Level" asks for one low photo near a place your cat uses. You are photographing the room, not their face, and the chair legs and doorway look completely different from down there. Our cat photoshoot ideas at home has more for when you want the cat in the frame.

And then the words. "The Names Used in This House" is a list of every name, nickname and silly phrase your household actually uses for the cat, with who says which. It takes five minutes, and the book asks you to be honest about the undignified ones too.

A paw print, only if your cat agrees

Paw prints are a common pet keepsake, and they can work for a cat who is relaxed about having their feet touched. Use a non-toxic product sold for pet prints, do it when your cat is calm, press once, and stop as soon as they pull away. Some cats will not want to, and a struggle is not worth a print.

Skip salt dough. The ASPCA says too much salt can cause vomiting, tremors, seizures and even death in pets, and home-made salt dough is mostly salt. A close photo of the paw where your cat is lying is a fine record on its own.

A cat keepsake box, and keepsake gifts

You do not need a special container. A shoebox, a small wooden box or a large envelope works, as long as everything inside is labelled:

  1. The whisker in its vial or card, with the date and place.
  2. An envelope of fur from the brush, dated.
  3. The retired collar and tag.
  4. The chosen object, if it is safe to keep.
  5. A few printed photos, including one from their level.
  6. The list of household names.

Keep the photos and sound files in one folder on your phone or computer, with a backup.

If you are looking for cat keepsake gifts for someone else, containers and help are the honest answer: a small wooden box, a few glass vials and labels, or an afternoon holding the phone while they photograph their cat's places. A printable book like ours is one more option. For a fuller record over time, a cat memory book or a simple cat behavior journal gives the box something to sit next to, and our things to do with your cat page has plenty more ways to spend an ordinary afternoon together.

Questions people ask

What are good cat keepsake ideas?

Things your cat already leaves around the house: a whisker that dropped out on its own, a tuft of fur from the brush or a blanket, a collar retired when it wore out, the box or blanket they claimed, a photo from their eye level, a few seconds of the sound they make arriving. Date each one and note where it came from.

What can I do with my cat's whiskers?

If you find one that has fallen out on its own, keep it somewhere it cannot bend: a small glass vial, a matchbox lined with paper, or taped inside a card with the date and where you found it. Some people later have one set into a pendant or a frame. Never cut or pull a whisker to get one.

What are good cat keepsake gifts?

For someone who lives with a cat, the useful gifts are containers and time: a small wooden box or glass vials for whiskers and fur, labels and envelopes, or an afternoon helping them photograph the cat's places. A printable keepsake book is another option.

Is it safe to make a paw print of my cat?

Only if your cat is relaxed about having their paws touched. Use a non-toxic product sold for pet prints and stop the moment they pull away. Avoid salt dough: the ASPCA says too much salt can poison pets. A close photo of the paw where they lie is a good alternative.
