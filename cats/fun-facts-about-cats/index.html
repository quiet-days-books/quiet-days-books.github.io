In short

  • Cats hear far higher sounds than we do, see better in dim light but not in total darkness, and rely on smell more than sight.
  • Most cats have 24 whiskers and 18 toes; the toe record is 29.
  • Cats can't taste sugar, lap about four times a second and groom with hollow spines on the tongue.
  • Cats are most active at dusk and dawn, not at night, and sleep around 12 to 18 hours a day.
  • Studies show cats know their own names, follow a person's gaze and often fetch untrained.

Cats hear sounds about three times higher than people can, can't taste sugar, usually have 24 whiskers and 18 toes, lap water about four times a second and can pick out their own name from other words. Below are 29 facts like these. We checked each one in a named source, and we left out popular "facts" we could not confirm.

They are grouped by the part of the cat you see every day: eyes and ears, whiskers, paws, tongue, sleep, voice, behaviour and history.

Fun facts about cats' senses

Start with the eyes, ears and nose.

  1. In dim light cats see far better than we do. Cats Protection puts it at six to eight times better, helped by about three times as many rod cells, the light-sensitive cells of the eye. In total darkness they can't see, though: Cats Protection calls that a myth.
  2. The shine of a cat's eyes at night comes from the tapetum, a reflective layer at the back of the eye that bounces light back onto the rod cells.
  3. Cats see fewer colours. Red and green look like shades of grey to them; they are much better with blue and yellow.
  4. A cat's field of view is about 200 degrees, against our 180.
  5. Cats struggle to focus on anything closer than about 30 centimetres. Up close they rely on their whiskers instead.
  6. People hear up to about 20,000 vibrations per second. Cats typically hear 60,000 or more, says the Cornell Feline Health Center.
  7. Each ear can rotate about 180 degrees, and sound reaching the two ears at slightly different times lets a cat pinpoint where it came from.
  8. For cats, smell matters more than sight. They also have a second scent organ, the vomeronasal or Jacobson's organ, between the roof of the mouth and the nostrils. The open-mouthed grimace a cat makes after sniffing something interesting, the Flehmen response, draws scent into it.

You will often read that a cat moves each ear with 32 muscles. We could not find that number in a veterinary or university source, so it is not on this list.

Cat whisker facts

  1. Most cats have 24 whiskers, 12 on each cheek, in four horizontal rows of three. Many also have whiskers above the eyes, on the jawline and on the back of the front legs.
  2. Whiskers are roughly as wide as the cat's body, so they work like a measuring tape for gaps.
  3. At the base of each whisker is a sensor that picks up touch and even air movement. That helps a cat find its way in the dark and sense things too close to see. Cats Protection says whiskers should never be trimmed or cut.

Fun facts about cats' paws

  1. Most cats have 18 toes: five on each front paw and four on each back paw. A cat with extra toes is called polydactyl.
  2. The record is 29 toes. Guinness World Records measured it on a cat called Erebus in Kaukauna, Wisconsin, on 13 May 2026.
  3. Cats can be left- or right-pawed. In a 2018 study of 44 cats published in Animal Behaviour, 73% showed a paw preference when reaching for food. Males tended to use the left paw and females the right.

Cat tongue and taste facts

  1. Cats can't taste sugar. A 2005 study found that Tas1r2, a gene needed for the sweet taste receptor, is broken in cats, so they lack the system to taste sugars.
  2. Cats don't scoop water. MIT researchers found that the tip of the tongue barely brushes the surface; pulling it back up draws a column of liquid into the mouth. A house cat does this about four times a second.
  3. The little spines on a cat's tongue are hollow at the tip. A 2018 study showed they wick saliva from the mouth and carry it into the fur when the cat grooms.

Cat sleep facts

  1. Domestic cats sleep around 12 to 18 hours a day, says Cats Protection, often in naps as short as 10 minutes.
  2. Cats are not nocturnal. They are crepuscular, the PDSA says: most active at dusk and dawn, when their prey is too.
  3. Cats have REM sleep, the dreaming stage in people. In the 1960s and 70s, the French scientist Michel Jouvet found that cats whose REM muscle paralysis was blocked stalked and groomed in their sleep. Our article do cats dream covers what that does and doesn't show, and why cats sleep so much explains the long hours.

Fun facts about cat behavior: voice, purring, play

  1. Purring is very low: about 20 to 30 vibrations per second. In 2023, University of Vienna researchers found that a cat's larynx can make purring sounds without a rhythmic nerve signal, possibly helped by a small pad in the vocal folds. Our guide on how to make a cat purr covers the everyday side.
  2. Meows are mainly for us. International Cat Care says that in most situations meows are directed at people rather than other cats.
  3. Cats know their names. A 2019 study in Scientific Reports found cats could tell their own name from other words, even when a stranger said it.
  4. Cats follow where you look. In a 2019 study of 41 cats, they found food hidden under the pot a person looked at 70% of the time. It is one reason a cat stares at you so much.
  5. Their short-term memory for a hidden object is short but real: in a 2006 study of 24 cats, accuracy dropped quickly after the object vanished but stayed above chance after 60 seconds. More in how good is a cat's memory.
  6. Plenty of cats fetch. Owners reported it in 40.9% of 8,224 cats in a 2024 study, and in a 2023 survey 94.4% of owners of fetching cats said nobody had trained it. See why your cat brings you toys.

Two more signals are worth knowing, though they belong to the body rather than the voice. A tail held straight up, a 2009 study concluded, signals an intention to interact in a friendly way; our guide to cat tail behavior meaning covers the rest of the tail. And a slow blink, according to 2020 research in Scientific Reports, appears to be a sign of positive emotion.

Cat history and record facts

Three more, from the long view:

  1. A 2007 genetic study of 979 domestic cats traced their origin to wildcats of the Near East, probably around the time farming villages appeared in the Fertile Crescent.
  2. Around 9,500 years ago, on Cyprus, a cat was buried just 40 centimetres from a human grave. Cats are not native to the island.
  3. The oldest cat on record, says Guinness World Records, was Creme Puff of Austin, Texas: born on 3 August 1967, she lived until 6 August 2005, 38 years and 3 days.

Cat facts you can check on your own cat

Some of these you can see at home without any equipment:

  1. Count the whiskers on one cheek. Twelve is typical.
  2. Count the toes on a front paw and a back paw, if your cat is happy to let you look.
  3. Watch which paw reaches first for a toy under the sofa. Note it a few times over a week.
  4. Say your cat's name among a few similar-sounding words and watch the ears and head.
  5. Look where your cat is at dawn and at dusk compared with the middle of the day.
  6. Watch a drinking cat closely from the side. The quick lapping is easier to see than you'd expect.

Questions people ask

What are some fun facts about cats?

Cats hear sounds up to about 60,000 vibrations per second, three times higher than people. They can't taste sugar because of a broken sweet-taste gene. Most have 24 whiskers and 18 toes. They lap about four times a second, and they can tell their own name from other words.

What are fun facts about cats' paws?

Most cats have 18 toes, five on each front paw and four on each back paw. The record is 29 toes, held by Erebus of Wisconsin since May 2026. In a 2018 study of 44 cats, males tended to use the left paw and females the right.

Can cats see in the dark?

Not in total darkness. Cats Protection calls that a myth. In dim light, though, cats see much better than we do, six to eight times better by its estimate, thanks to more light-sensitive rod cells and a reflective layer behind the retina that makes their eyes shine.

Are cats nocturnal?

No. Cats are crepuscular, the PDSA says, meaning they are most active at dusk and dawn, when their prey is most active too. That is why many cats are busiest early in the morning and in the evening, and sleep through much of the day and night.

What is the oldest cat ever?

Guinness World Records lists Creme Puff of Austin, Texas, as the oldest cat ever. She was born on 3 August 1967 and lived until 6 August 2005, an age of 38 years and 3 days.
