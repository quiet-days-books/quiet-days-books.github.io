In short

  • Most cats sleep 12 to 18 hours a day; the PDSA puts the average at about 15, and some cats sleep up to 20.
  • Cats are hunters: hunting takes bursts of energy, and sleep is how they build it back.
  • They are crepuscular, busiest at dawn and dusk, so the middle of the day is nap time.
  • Much of it is light dozing in short spells; deep sleep is only about a quarter of the total.
  • Indoor cats and older cats tend to sleep more; outdoor cats sleep less because they spend more time exploring.

Cats sleep so much because they are hunters built for short bursts of effort. Hunting uses a lot of energy, and sleep is how a cat builds it back. Most cats sleep 12 to 18 hours a day, about 15 on average, much of it in light dozes, and they save their busy spells for dawn and dusk.

How many hours a day do cats sleep?

Vet and welfare organisations give slightly different figures, but they overlap:

Source Hours of sleep a day
VCA Animal Hospitals 12 to 16
Cats Protection about 12 to 18
PDSA about 15 on average, some cats up to 20

Age moves a cat around in that range. Cats Protection and the PDSA both say very young cats and older cats sleep more than the average adult, and the PDSA puts them closer to 20 hours.

So a cat curled up for most of the afternoon is well within the normal range.

Why do cats sleep so much?

The short answer is hunting. The PDSA explains that during a hunt a cat uses a lot of energy to stay alert, and that sleep is the only way for it to build that energy back up and save it for the next hunt. Cats Protection says the same from the other side: a tired cat with no energy cannot hunt or protect itself.

A pet cat with a full bowl still runs on that plan. VCA Animal Hospitals puts it plainly: pampered or not, cats keep the instincts of lone hunters. Without a group to keep watch, a cat that sleeps needs a place where nothing can creep up on it, which is why so many naps happen up high or tucked away. Our page on cat sleeping spots covers what each choice of place says.

Why do cats sleep so much during the day?

Because their busy hours are at the edges of it. Cats are crepuscular, most active at dusk and dawn, the PDSA says, and that comes from their hunting instinct. The middle of the day is the natural time to rest.

Cats Protection describes cats resting or sleeping on and off throughout the day rather than in one long block. So the day of a house cat often looks like this: a lively spell early, a long series of naps through the middle, another lively spell as the light goes, and more naps after. Our page on the cat daily routine looks at that pattern and how cats fit it around their people.

Light sleep and deep sleep

Much of what looks like sleep is a doze. The PDSA describes cats either dozing in a light, non-REM sleep or sleeping very deeply. When dozing, a cat is in what the PDSA calls "standby mode", still alert to what is going on around it.

The PDSA's figures for one cycle:

  • a light sleep usually lasts 15 to 30 minutes
  • then comes a deep sleep of typically 5 to 7 minutes, when a cat is much harder to wake
  • around 25% of all sleeping time is very deep sleep

That is why a cat can look fast asleep and still turn an ear towards a sound. Cats Protection adds that when a cat is deeply asleep you may see its mouth or paws twitch, which is normal and probably dreaming. What research says about cat dreams is on our page do cats dream.

Why do indoor cats sleep so much?

Often because there is less to do. The PDSA says outdoor cats typically sleep less than house cats, because they spend more time exploring their surroundings. It also says that if a cat lacks mental stimulation, it will often resort to a nap.

That does not mean an indoor cat that sleeps a lot is unhappy. It does mean a little play can change the shape of its day. A few ideas:

  1. Play just before the cat's natural busy times, early morning and early evening.
  2. Use a wand toy that moves like prey, and let the cat catch it at the end.
  3. Keep sessions short, a few minutes, and stop while the cat still wants more.
  4. Give it a window with something to watch.
  5. Rotate toys so the same ones do not go stale.

Why do old cats sleep so much?

Older cats tire out faster. Cats Protection says their bodies need more rest, because a lot of physical activity is more strenuous for them. The PDSA puts older cats closer to 20 hours of sleep a day.

So an older cat that sleeps most of the day is usually just being an older cat. Make its favourite places easy to reach and warm, and let it choose. If your cat suddenly sleeps much more than it used to, Cats Protection and the PDSA both suggest checking with your vet.

Why do cats sleep so much in winter?

The PDSA says cats tend to avoid bad weather in the colder seasons and prefer to get extra sleep. Cats Protection notes that cats like to sleep somewhere warm, comfortable, high up and safe, and that in summer a cat may prefer to sleep outdoors in the sun. As the seasons change, so do the favourite places, and often the amount of time spent in them.

Noticing where your cat sleeps

A cat that sleeps 15 hours a day spends most of its life in a handful of places, and they change with the light and the season. Cats Protection notes that cats often rotate their preferred sleeping area.

Our book The House Your Cat Knows has a page for this called "The Nap Rotation": over one day or one week, write down two or three places your cat rests, and what the light, heat or noise is like in each. The book's rule for it is the right one for any sleeping cat: do not wake your cat, use flash or rearrange the light to get the note or the photo. If you only catch one nap, one is enough.

Questions people ask

How many hours a day do cats sleep?

About 12 to 18 hours, says Cats Protection, and VCA Animal Hospitals gives 12 to 16. The PDSA puts the average at about 15 hours, with some cats sleeping up to 20. Very young and older cats sleep at the top of that range.

Why do cats sleep so much during the day?

Cats are crepuscular, which means they are most active at dawn and dusk, according to the PDSA. That leaves the middle of the day, and much of the night, for rest. A cat that naps all afternoon is often saving its energy for the evening.

Why do indoor cats sleep so much?

Partly because there is less to do. The PDSA says outdoor cats typically sleep less than house cats because they spend more time exploring, and that a cat without enough mental stimulation will often resort to a nap. Short play sessions that copy a hunt help.

Why do old cats sleep so much?

Older cats tire out faster, and Cats Protection says their bodies need more rest because a lot of physical activity is more strenuous for them. They often sleep closer to 20 hours a day. Warm, easy-to-reach resting places help an older cat settle.

Are cats really asleep when they doze?

Often only lightly. The PDSA describes cats dozing in a 'standby mode', still alert to what is around them. A light sleep usually lasts 15 to 30 minutes before a deep sleep of about 5 to 7 minutes, and deep sleep makes up around 25% of the total.
