The best cat Christmas photos come from what your cat already does when the house changes: the spot they claim under the tree, the delivery box they move into, the window where the lights reflect. Set the camera at their level, turn the flash off and wait for them. Leave hats and sweaters out, and keep lilies, tinsel and glass baubles off the set.

A cat can't be posed the way a dog can, and trying usually ends the session. So the ideas below start from the cat's choices, and you bring the Christmas to them.

Cat Christmas photo ideas that start with your cat

When the decorations go up, your cat will probably inspect them. That inspection is the photo.

  • The new spot. Once the tree is up, your cat may pick a place near it or under it. Photograph them there, from the floor, lights soft behind them.
  • The box. December brings deliveries, and your cat may claim one of the boxes. Our book's activity Their Chosen Object asks you to photograph the object your cat chooses again and again, and to write how long it has been part of the house. A cat in a plain cardboard box next to the tree is a Christmas card.
  • The window. The Window They Choose is another activity in our book: photograph the window your cat already uses and note what the light is doing. In December, add fairy lights in the room so they reflect in the glass behind the cat.
  • The usual place, decorated. Put a string of lights near the chair or bed your cat already sleeps on, not on it, and photograph the cat as you find them.
  • The tree from their level. Get down low and shoot up through the lower branches toward the cat. A Photo From Their Level is an activity in our book for exactly this kind of angle.
  • Unwrapping. Your cat watching paper come off a present, or sitting in the pile afterwards. Clear away ribbon and string straight after.

Each of these is in our book The House Your Cat Knows, in everyday form. Christmas just changes the room around them.

Why we skip hats, sweaters and antlers

Costumes appear in most cat Christmas lists. Cats Protection strongly advises against dressing cats in any form of clothing or accessories, and notes that costumes can restrict movement. That is reason enough for us.

You can get the same effect without touching the cat:

  • A red or plaid blanket the cat already uses, washed and put back in the usual spot.
  • Your own Christmas jumper in the frame, with the cat beside you.
  • Out-of-focus lights or the tree behind them.
  • A single bauble (plastic) in the foreground, out of reach.

Cat photo Christmas card ideas

A card photo has to read at small size and leave room for words.

  1. Choose the shape first. Square or portrait suits most cards. Turn the phone before you start.
  2. Leave empty space above or beside the cat for the greeting.
  3. Put the cat side-on to a window. Turn the flash off.
  4. Use burst mode and take many frames. Cats blink and turn at the wrong moment.
  5. Pick the photo that looks like your cat: the bent whisker, the expression you know.

Good card subjects: asleep under the tree, curled in a box beside the presents, at the window with lights in the glass, or a close-up of eyes and whiskers with the lights blurred behind.

Christmas props: what is safe near a cat

Some popular props are on hazard lists. This table is from Cats Protection's Christmas advice and the ASPCA's holiday safety page.

Prop or decoration Use it? Why
Lilies in a bouquet No Very dangerous to cats, even small amounts of pollen (Cats Protection); can cause kidney failure (ASPCA)
Mistletoe, holly No Can cause stomach upset and heart problems if eaten (ASPCA)
Tinsel, ribbon, gift ribbon No Harmful if swallowed or caught around the neck or legs (Cats Protection)
Glass baubles No Shatter easily (Cats Protection)
Snow globe No Contains ethylene glycol, highly toxic to pets (ASPCA)
Fairy lights Behind the cat, wires covered Lights should be used safely, switched off at the mains when nobody is home (Cats Protection)
Candles No Never leave candles unattended (ASPCA)
A plain box, their own blanket Yes Familiar and safe

Cats Protection also suggests a sturdy base for the tree and a quiet room or hiding place the cat can escape to while the house is busy. If you think your cat has eaten something toxic, contact your vet.

When to stop

International Cat Care describes a relaxed cat as one with ears facing forward, a tail held up, soft eyes with blinking, lying or sitting with some paws off the ground. A fearful cat may have ears flat against the head and wide pupils, a tail tucked between the back legs, a body held low with all four paws on the floor, or look away and blink rapidly.

If you see the second set, put the phone down. And if the cat walks away, the session is over. Try again another day; the tree will still be there.

Phone tips for Christmas light

December daylight is short and the tree lights are bright, which confuses a phone camera.

  • Shoot in the late morning or early afternoon, when the window light is strongest.
  • Put the cat near the window and the tree behind them, not the other way round.
  • Tap the cat's face to focus. Then slide exposure down until the lights stop glowing white, and up again until you can see fur.
  • With a black cat, favour more window light and take the exposure up a little more; a dark cat in front of bright lights can come out as a shape with no detail.
  • Portrait mode blurs the lights into soft circles. Check the edges around whiskers, which it sometimes cuts off.

Our guide to cat photoshoot ideas at home has more on phone settings and on photographing your cat without props all year.

Cats at Christmas: picture ideas beyond the tree

Some of the best December photos have no decorations in them at all.

  • The radiator or heater spot your cat uses all winter.
  • Your cat watching guests arrive from the stairs or a high shelf.
  • The cat asleep on the coats piled on the bed.
  • An empty box with the cat's shape still in it, for when they are elsewhere.
  • Your cat with the person they choose when the house is full. Our book's activity The Person They Choose asks you to record one ordinary moment of chosen company.

If your cat disappears under a bed while the house is busy, leave them there and don't follow with the camera. That place is theirs. Our guide to where cats sleep and what it means covers why those spots matter.

After Christmas

Pick one photo for the card, then keep the rest with a date and a line about where the cat was. Next December, try to take the same photo in the same place. For more ways to spend winter afternoons with your cat, see our list of things to do with your cat.

Questions people ask

How do I take a Christmas photo of my cat?

Find the place your cat already uses near the decorations, set the phone at their eye level with the tree lights behind them, turn off the flash, and wait. Take short sessions on several days rather than one long one, and stop when your cat walks away.

Can I put a Santa hat on my cat?

Cats Protection strongly advises against dressing cats in any clothing or accessories, including hats and antlers. A red blanket your cat already sleeps on, or the tree behind them, says Christmas without asking anything of the cat.

What Christmas decorations are dangerous for cats?

Lilies and even small amounts of their pollen (Cats Protection), tinsel and ribbon, glass baubles, snow globes, which contain ethylene glycol (ASPCA), mistletoe and holly, and chewable light wires. If you think your cat has eaten something toxic, contact your vet straight away.

How do I photograph a black cat with Christmas lights?

Put the cat side-on to a window so daylight falls on the face, and keep the tree lights behind them as a blurred background. On a phone, tap the cat's face to focus and slide exposure down until the lights stop burning out, then up again until you see fur.

What are good cat photo Christmas card ideas?

A cat asleep under the tree, a cat in the box they claimed from the deliveries, a cat at the window with lights reflected in the glass, or a close-up of eyes and whiskers with lights blurred behind. Leave empty space for the greeting.
